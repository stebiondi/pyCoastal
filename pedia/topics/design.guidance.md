## Synthesis

**Well established.** Coastal design guidance converts hazard and performance evidence into repeatable decisions. Credible guidance states scope, assumptions, failure modes, uncertainty, safety and serviceability criteria, maintenance, monitoring, and consequences rather than presenting coefficients without provenance.

**Governing physics.** Guidance must connect waves, water levels, tsunami, erosion, scour, overtopping, structural resistance, ecological response, human use, deterioration, and adaptation because performance and failure emerge from interacting loads and systems.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Process-specific controls include relative freeboard, surf similarity, stability and scour numbers, while guidance-level controls include return period, target probability, reliability index, consequence class, safety factor, discount rate, service life, and performance-indicator normalization.

**Major equations.** Common design frameworks combine joint probability and return levels, limit states and partial factors, runup and overtopping relations, stability and scour formulae, reliability or risk integrals, lifecycle cost, multi-criteria decisions, and adaptive pathways.

**Typical methods.** Methods synthesize codes, field failures, laboratory and numerical models, expert judgment, probabilistic analysis, consequence assessment, lifecycle and cost-benefit methods, multi-criteria analysis, monitoring, and periodic revision.

**Numerical models.** Guidance-supporting tools include joint-probability extreme-level models, spectral wave and overtopping models, XBeach with multilevel Monte Carlo, performance-indicator frameworks, and multi-hazard risk models.

**Experimental datasets.** Reviewed sources draw on post-tsunami surveys, 187 hybrid-measure studies, eight expert interviews, NNBF literature and experts, and global adaptation portfolios; adjacent evidence provides joint sea levels, port overtopping, and hydro-morphodynamic uncertainty.

**Validated ranges.** This abstract-bounded slice supports no universal coefficient range. Quantitative adjacent evidence includes 40-fold-or-greater MLMC speedups and a 187-study hybrid assessment base; every formula must retain its source-specific validity limits.

**Recent advances.** Recent guidance emphasizes portfolios, nature-based and hybrid infrastructure, uncertainty-efficient ensembles, integrated earthquake-tsunami assessment, broader performance indicators, and explicit links between structural and evacuation measures.

**Disagreements.** Hard, nature-based, hybrid, and nonstructural measures are not interchangeable. Structural measures can delay tsunami inundation yet fail without geometry, construction and maintenance; hybrid ambitions often exceed the hazard-focused indicators currently used to evaluate them.

**Limitations.** Guidance can lag climate, technology, and evidence; transfer across morphology and governance can fail. Sparse extremes, correlated loads, uncertain deterioration, ecological dynamics, maintenance, informal development, and inconsistent indicators constrain standardization.

**Open questions.** Needs include climate-adjusted and adaptive standards, transparent hybrid indicators, multi-hazard sequences, lifecycle reliability, monitored performance, equitable consequences, update triggers, and machine-readable provenance for every recommendation.

**Seminal papers.** The branch rests on probabilistic coastal levels, empirical structure manuals, reliability and limit-state design, risk-based planning, and field-forensic learning; current sources extend these toward NNBF, hybrid, and multi-hazard guidance.

## Claims

- **C400.** A literature-and-expert synthesis concludes that emergent vegetation can be designed with current evidence but requires a risk-based wave-attenuation framework that makes calculation uncertainty and engineering, ecological, and social implementation barriers explicit. *Regime: Emergent vegetation systems used as coastal infrastructure..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Kayla Ostrow 2022, [doi:10.3389/fbuil.2022.923965](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbuil.2022.923965))
- **C401.** Post-tsunami field evidence indicates that seawalls, breakwaters, and canals can reduce tsunami energy and delay inundation, but reliable mitigation depends on geometry, construction quality, maintenance, and integration with evacuation rather than structure presence alone. *Regime: Seawalls, breakwaters, and water-filled canals across major tsunami events including 2011 Tohoku..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Reza Arefi 2026, [doi:10.3390/fluids11080188](https://doi.org/10.3390/fluids11080188))
- **C402.** Across 187 studies and eight expert interviews, hybrid flood-measure assessment was dominated by hydraulic and cost metrics, while long-term adaptation, component interaction, ecological development, maintenance, and governance effectiveness were rarely evaluated together. *Regime: Grey-green-blue hybrid measures across pluvial, fluvial, and coastal flooding..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Borjana Bogatinoska 2026, [doi:10.1088/1748-9326/ae7988](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ae7988))
- **C403.** An integrated coastal-structure risk framework treats earthquake and tsunami demands jointly, addressing the vulnerability created when seismic damage changes structural capacity before tsunami loading. *Regime: Coastal structures and communities exposed to sequential or combined earthquake and tsunami hazards..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Cláudia Reis 2022, [doi:10.1016/j.rcns.2022.07.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rcns.2022.07.001))
- **C404.** A global adaptation synthesis finds that flood-risk practice has shifted from structure-only modification toward portfolios that include community-centered and nature-based measures, supporting guidance based on complementary rather than isolated interventions. *Regime: Flood adaptation measures under changing climate risk..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Mohammed Azhar 2025, [doi:10.1038/s43247-025-02779-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-025-02779-z))
- **C1410.** A coastal-restoration synthesis identifies research priorities and practitioner motivations needed to align project design, monitoring, ecosystem recovery and stakeholder outcomes. *Regime: Priorities and Motivations of Marine Coastal Restoration Research.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Elisa Bayraktarov 2020, [doi:10.3389/fmars.2020.00484](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2020.00484))
- **C1471.** A historical synthesis traces coastal-defense development and the changing engineering responses to sea-level variation and shoreline retreat. *Regime: Panorama of the History of Coastal Protection.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Roger H. Charlier 2005, [doi:10.2112/03561.1](https://doi.org/10.2112/03561.1))
