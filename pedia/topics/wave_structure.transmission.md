## Synthesis

**Well established.** Wave transmission and reflection depend jointly on relative geometry, permeability, internal barriers, structural motion, wavelength, incidence direction, and spectral character; a coefficient calibrated for one structure family should not be transferred without checking its validity regime.

**Governing physics.** Fixed submerged structures redistribute incident energy through reflection, transmission, breaking and porous dissipation, while floating structures add heave and other motion-coupled pathways; periodic arrays can also create Bragg resonance.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Important controls in this evidence slice include porosity, relative submergence and crest geometry, kd, wavelength-to-structure scale, wave incidence, and the ratio of incident peak period to approximate natural heave period.

**Major equations.** Reviewed approaches include empirical hyperbolic-tangent transmission relations, Macagno-type fixed-box relations modified for floating heave, linear-wave eigenfunction matching, oblique-wave Bragg conditions, and database-based intercomparison of low-crested-structure formulae.

**Typical methods.** Typical methods combine regular- and irregular-wave flumes, three-dimensional basins, incident/reflected wave separation, motion and mooring measurements, analytical matching or shallow-water solutions, finite-volume and potential-flow models, and formula intercomparison against harmonized experimental databases.

**Numerical models.** The reviewed numerical approaches span linear eigenfunction matching for periodic structures, boundary-discretization models for pontoon arrays, finite-volume shallow-water solvers for rectangular bars, fully nonlinear potential-flow models for WEC breakwaters, and empirical transmission predictors.

**Experimental datasets.** The reviewed abstract-level datasets include porous models with porosity 0.40-0.80, eight chain-moored pi-type models of 16-76 kg, a ten-unit floating system in a 36 m by 60 m by 1.5 m basin, and trapezoidal impermeable, permeable and perforated submerged structures. Reviewed datasets include porous models with porosity 0.40-0.80, eight chain-moored pi-type models of 16-76 kg, a ten-unit floating system in a 36 m by 60 m by 1.5 m basin, four-configuration regular- and long-wave flume comparisons, and trapezoidal impermeable, permeable and perforated submerged structures.

**Validated ranges.** Directly reported bounds include porosity 0.40-0.80 with reflection coefficients 0.01-0.31 for non-breaking porous-model tests and masses of 16-76 kg for eight pi-type floating-breakwater models; other numerical ranges require full-text review.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances couple attenuation with wave-energy conversion, explore nonuniform-draft and hybrid OWC/OB systems, resolve resonant transmission-motion-load tradeoffs, and optimize internal screens, porosity, and periodic geometry using combined experiments and nonlinear models.

**Disagreements.** Apparent design rankings depend on the objective: a porous configuration can transmit more energy yet reduce motions and mooring loads, while mesh or internal barriers can improve attenuation but increase selected loads. These are regime-dependent tradeoffs rather than universally contradictory results.

**Limitations.** The present synthesis is abstract-limited. Scale effects, exact wave ranges, coefficient uncertainty, structural damage, prototype bathymetry, and field validation remain unresolved, and linear Bragg results apply only under small-amplitude assumptions. Most sources remain abstract-limited, with three lawful accepted manuscripts reviewed in full. Scale effects, exact wave ranges, coefficient uncertainty, structural damage, prototype bathymetry, and field validation remain unresolved, and linear Bragg results apply only under small-amplitude assumptions.

**Open questions.** Priority needs are independent cross-formula benchmarks, prototype validation under irregular multidirectional seas, uncertainty-aware extrapolation limits, and coupled assessment of transmission, reflection, dissipation, structural motion, loads and morphology.

**Seminal papers.** Within this bounded corpus, early permeable-breakwater and low-crested formula families establish the design lineage, while later harmonized reviews make their calibration and extrapolation limits explicit; the present slice does not yet support a definitive field-wide priority ranking.

## Claims

- **C344.** For the tested submerged trapezoidal structures, adding an impermeable vertical screen inside a perforated hollow structure reduced wave transmission substantially relative to a perforated structure without the screen, whereas differences between impermeable and permeable structures were comparatively limited. *Regime: Trapezoidal impermeable, permeable, and perforated submerged structures; abstract-level evidence only..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Marcel R.A. van Gent 2023, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2023.104344](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2023.104344))
- **C345.** A harmonized experimental database permits transmission formulae for submerged and low-crested breakwaters to be compared both within and beyond their original validity limits; agreement reported for one formula within its calibration range is not evidence of reliable extrapolation. *Regime: Submerged and low-crested breakwaters represented in the collected experimental database; abstract-level evidence does not report numerical validity bounds..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Alberica Brancasi 2022, [doi:10.3390/jmse10121986](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse10121986))
- **C346.** Under the study's small-amplitude, linear-wave assumptions, eigenfunction-matching calculations for periodic surface-piercing and submerged structures found that oblique-wave Bragg's law predicted the occurrence of Bragg resonance. *Regime: Small-amplitude oblique waves interacting with periodic surface-piercing or submerged structures over step-approximated periodic bottoms..* [direct_finding, mixed] (I-Fan Tseng 2020, [doi:10.3390/jmse8070522](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse8070522))
- **C347.** For hollow framework submerged-breakwater models with porosity from 0.40 to 0.80 under non-breaking waves, transmission increased with porosity, measured reflection coefficients ranged from 0.01 to 0.31, and primary-wave energy loss was greatest near kd=1.9. *Regime: Non-breaking waves over hollow framework submerged-breakwater models with porosity 0.40-0.80..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Faridah Jaffar Sidek 2018, [doi:10.11113/mjce.v19.15744](https://doi.org/10.11113/mjce.v19.15744))
- **C348.** For tested chain-moored pi-type floating breakwaters, an empirical transmission predictor based on the ratio of incident peak period to approximate natural heave period corrected the fixed-box Macagno relationship and showed fairly good agreement with the study and literature datasets. *Regime: Eight chain-moored pi-type floating-breakwater models of 16-76 kg plus literature tests under regular and irregular waves..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Piero Ruol 2012, [doi:10.1061/(asce)ww.1943-5460.0000153](https://doi.org/10.1061/(asce)ww.1943-5460.0000153))
- **C349.** For the tested ten-unit taut-moored cylindrical floating-breakwater system, both transmission coefficient and motion-response amplitude increased with wavelength to a peak and then decreased under the examined oblique and beam sea conditions. *Regime: A ten-unit cylindrical floating-breakwater system with mesh cages and taut moorings under oblique and beam seas in a 36 m by 60 m by 1.5 m basin..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Chunyan Ji 2016, [doi:10.1016/j.oceaneng.2016.07.051](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2016.07.051))
- **C351.** For the tested hybrid oscillating-buoy/oscillating-water-column breakwater, the combined system improved both conversion and attenuation relative to isolated devices; gap resonance reduced extraction but had negligible attenuation effect, and OWC PTO damping primarily controlled transmission. *Regime: Hybrid upstream oscillating buoy and downstream oscillating-water-column floating breakwater under the tested wave, draft, gap, and PTO conditions..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Yong Cheng 2022, [doi:10.1016/j.rser.2022.112909](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2022.112909))
- **C352.** Within the tested equal-displacement comparison, a nonuniform-draft oscillating-water-column dual-pontoon breakwater provided better attenuation and conversion than the oscillating-buoy single pontoon, while increasing chamber-width ratio improved attenuation but reduced maximum conversion efficiency. *Regime: Compared single-pontoon oscillating-buoy and dual-pontoon oscillating-water-column floating breakwaters over tested geometry, gap, wave, and PTO ranges..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Yong Cheng 2022, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2022.104188](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2022.104188))
- **C353.** For the modeled fixed floating-pontoon trains, Bragg-reflection coefficients depended on breakwater spacing, installed pontoon count, draft, width, and wavelength, with the numerical model agreeing with analytical literature benchmarks. *Regime: Fixed floating-pontoon trains over the modeled spacing, count, draft, width, and wavelength combinations..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Huei-Tau Ouyang 2015, [doi:10.1515/ijnaoe-2015-0066](https://doi.org/10.1515/ijnaoe-2015-0066))
- **C354.** For rectangular submerged bars under the study's shallow-water and monochromatic-wave assumptions, analytical and finite-volume solutions were used with experimental data to identify bar height and length combinations that minimize transmission. *Regime: Monochromatic waves passing one or multiple rectangular submerged bars under shallow-water-equation assumptions..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Ikha Magdalena 2020, [doi:10.3390/computation8020056](https://doi.org/10.3390/computation8020056))
- **C355.** In the four-model regular-wave comparison, the cylindrical floating breakwater with mesh cage and suspended balls produced the strongest attenuation, particularly for long and high waves; the mesh reduced heave and roll but increased sway and could raise short-period windward mooring tension. *Regime: Four floating-breakwater models under regular waves, including cylindrical pontoons with a mesh cage and suspended balls; accepted manuscript reviewed in full..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Chunyan Ji 2015, [doi:10.1016/j.oceaneng.2015.06.046](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2015.06.046))
- **C356.** Across four regular-wave floating-breakwater configurations, mesh-cage type I reflected and dissipated waves most efficiently, whereas the porous model admitted more transmitted energy but produced the smallest heave, roll, and mooring forces. *Regime: Cylindrical, porous, and two mesh-cage floating-breakwater configurations under tested regular waves; accepted manuscript reviewed in full..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Chunyan Ji 2016, [doi:10.1016/j.oceaneng.2016.03.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2016.03.002))
- **C357.** For four slack-moored floating-breakwater models in long regular waves, transmission and dissipation exhibited turning points near structural natural periods; porous plates and mesh cages added mass and damping that mitigated these features, while resonance coupled attenuation to motions and mooring loads. *Regime: Four slack-moored single-box, porous-box, double-pontoon, and mesh-cage floating breakwaters under long regular waves; accepted manuscript reviewed in full..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Chunyan Ji 2018, [doi:10.1016/j.oceaneng.2018.01.055](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2018.01.055))
