# Tsunami loads and debris

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Tsunami loads combine hydrostatic and drag-like demand with short impulsive/slamming peaks, uplift and overturning, trapped-air effects, and stochastic debris collision; local pressures and component load paths can govern even when total force appears moderate.

**Governing physics.** Load histories depend on bore depth, velocity and relative depth, wave breaking and duration, deck clearance and geometry, air compression and escape, structural motion, debris buoyancy/trajectory/contact, added water mass, and the superposition of debris and hydrodynamic force.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include Froude number, relative bore depth, wave height and wavelength relative to deck width/clearance, blockage and girder count, air-volume and compressibility ratios, debris density and size ratios, impact velocity, stiffness/contact-time ratios, and vegetation submergence.

**Major equations.** Assessment uses depth-integrated momentum and drag/hydrostatic terms, pressure integration, rigid-body and finite-element response, RANS or Green–Naghdi flow, SPH fluid–solid coupling, contact impulse and added-mass models, and probabilistic collision/load descriptions.

**Typical methods.** Typical methods are dam-break and pneumatic long-wave experiments, force/pressure and high-speed-motion measurement, solitary-wave and transient-bore comparison, SPH–multibody and CFD–FE coupling, ALE multiphase models, surrogate response surfaces, and critical evidence reviews.

**Numerical models.** Validated tools include GPUSPH, DualSPHysics–Project Chrono, DualSPHysics–Abaqus, compressible ALE, Navier–Stokes/RANS, Green–Naghdi and Saint-Venant equations, plus PCE, ANN, and Kriging load surrogates.

**Experimental datasets.** The branch includes 1:5 flexible bridge tests, laboratory debris and dam-break benchmarks, open-girder deck validation, pneumatic reproduction of the 2004 and 2011 tsunami records, bore-pressure comparisons, and vegetation-breakage force tests.

**Validated ranges.** Reported benchmarks include trapped-air uplift increases of 39% on average and 148% maximum, a 32% average overturning increase, oblique-wave forces of 1901 kN horizontal and 862 kN longitudinal, prototype-equivalent tsunami durations of 2–20 min, and intact-vegetation force reduction up to 70%.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances combine open-source SPH with nonsmooth multibody contact and stochastic initial conditions, high-fidelity deck-geometry and oblique-wave studies, probabilistic debris-collision reviews, and vegetation-breakage-aware mitigation experiments.

**Disagreements.** Simplified design equations can be non-conservative; solitary waves and transient bores do not always reproduce the same load path; trapped air can raise or lower slamming while increasing quasi-static uplift; and increased girder count can amplify or reduce force depending on wave regime.

**Limitations.** Reduced-scale wave generation, air scaling, rigid-body assumptions, idealized debris/contact, sparse full-scale records, uncertain collision probability, limited debris ensembles and damming data, and model-dependent turbulence or pressure peaks restrict generalization.

**Open questions.** Priorities are probabilistic debris fields, multiple impacts and damming, deformable and negatively buoyant debris, air–water–structure coupling, structural motion/failure, scour and foundation interaction, transferable scaling, and code-level uncertainty factors.

**Seminal papers.** The evidence pathway progresses from physical bore and single-debris impact tests and simplified design formulas to three-dimensional pressure fields, flexible bridge response, realistic pneumatic tsunami histories, and coupled fluid–multibody contact.

## Claims

- **C633.** Tsunami debris-load research is most mature for a single impacting object, with ensemble motion, damming, collision probability, and stochastic load variability still unresolved. *Regime: Single-object impact is the best-developed case, while debris ensembles, damming, collision probability, and load variability remain major gaps..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Ioan Nistor 2017, [doi:10.3389/fbuil.2017.00002](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbuil.2017.00002))
- **C634.** Validated SPH simulations found that an upwave service bridge and breakwater can reduce tsunami force on a main bridge, while transferring severe demand to the shielding structures. *Regime: A two-girder service-road bridge and an offshore breakwater reduced load on the main bridge, with an optimum breakwater distance, but the shielding structures themselves received strong loads..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Zhangping Wei 2016, [doi:10.1007/s40722-016-0054-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40722-016-0054-6))
- **C635.** In 1:5 bridge tests, trapped air increased total uplift by 39% on average (148% maximum) and overturning moment by 32% on average, while redistributing component demand. *Regime: Trapped air increased total uplift by 39% on average and 148% at maximum and overturning moment by 32% on average; solitary-wave and bore effects differed..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Denis Istrati 2019, [doi:10.3390/geosciences9040191](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences9040191))
- **C636.** Three-dimensional SPH–FE analysis showed localized tsunami-bore pressure peaks and found commonly used design-pressure equations could be highly non-conservative. *Regime: Peak pressures were localized and commonly used design equations could be highly non-conservative..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Gede Pringgana 2016, [doi:10.1680/jencm.15.00020](https://doi.org/10.1680/jencm.15.00020))
- **C637.** DualSPHysics–Chrono reproduced laboratory buoyant-container trajectories and velocities, supporting coupled flow–debris simulation before impact-load prediction. *Regime: The coupled model reproduced measured container centroid trajectory and velocity with quantitatively assessed agreement..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Gioele Ruffini 2021, [doi:10.3390/app11083618](https://doi.org/10.3390/app11083618))
- **C638.** Validated ALE simulations showed girder count can either raise or lower deck forces depending on wave size through acceleration, eddy dissipation, and pressure-phase effects. *Regime: Girder count changed force non-monotonically with wave height; chamber eddies and out-of-phase pressures weakened impacts for larger girder counts under large waves..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Tao Xiang 2021, [doi:10.3390/jmse9121342](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse9121342))
- **C639.** Oblique-wave CFD predicted forces up to 1901 kN horizontally and 862 kN longitudinally, with direction-dependent surrogate-model performance. *Regime: Extreme cases reached 1901 kN horizontal and 862 kN longitudinal force; third-order PCE best predicted vertical/horizontal loads and ANN longitudinal loads..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Lei Jia 2022, [doi:10.3390/jmse10070860](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse10070860))
- **C640.** Pneumatic simulators reproduced recorded tsunami histories at 1:50 scale for prototype-equivalent durations of 2–20 minutes, enabling more realistic structural-load experiments. *Regime: The systems reproduced recorded 2004 and 2011 tsunami histories at 1:50 scale over 20–240 s model periods, equivalent to 2–20 min prototype durations..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Ian Chandler 2021, [doi:10.3389/fbuil.2021.674659](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbuil.2021.674659))
- **C641.** Laboratory-compared Navier–Stokes results support breaking solitary waves and dam-break bores as useful, though distinct, proxies for tsunami deck loads. *Regime: Breaking solitary waves and dam-break bores produced reasonably representative tsunami loading conclusions across deck elevations..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Jiaqi Liu 2023, [doi:10.3390/jmse11051033](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse11051033))
- **C642.** Bore pressure comparisons found relative depth controls impact and Green–Naghdi can approach RANS results at much lower cost, while Saint-Venant substantially simplifies the physics. *Regime: Relative depth controlled bore shape, stability, and impact; Green–Naghdi results approached RANS at far lower cost, whereas Saint-Venant omitted substantial physics..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Jiaqi Liu 2019, [doi:10.1115/1.4044988](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4044988))
- **C643.** Validated SPH–multibody coupling showed nonsmooth contact and randomized initial debris position are essential to reproduce debris-impact forces and their stochastic variability. *Regime: Nonsmooth contact matched measurements and avoided numerical high-frequency noise; varying initial debris position reproduced experimental variability and exposed design-equation issues..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Ishiki 2025, [doi:10.1061/jwped5.wweng-2157](https://doi.org/10.1061/jwped5.wweng-2157))
- **C644.** Debris-collision assessment must jointly estimate collision probability, velocity, and force, with probabilistic evaluation the leading unresolved need. *Regime: Available methods trade accuracy, computation, and practicality; probabilistic evaluation remains the principal development need..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Kaida 2024, [doi:10.1080/21664250.2024.2341471](https://doi.org/10.1080/21664250.2024.2341471))
- **C645.** Laboratory tests directly evaluated tsunami waterborne-debris impact forces against FEMA P-646 and P-55 design formulations. *Regime: Experiments quantified debris impact forces and evaluated prescribed guideline formulas..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Omolbanin Farahmandpour 2016, [doi:10.21660/2016.22.69033](https://doi.org/10.21660/2016.22.69033))
- **C646.** Tsunami structural-load assessment relies on complementary physical experiments and numerical simulation, with design-guideline revision requiring validated use of both. *Regime: Physical testing and numerical simulation are complementary, and numerical models can represent parameters omitted from reduced physical tests..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Nelvindran Govindasamy 2018, [doi:10.14419/ijet.v7i4.35.22752](https://doi.org/10.14419/ijet.v7i4.35.22752))
- **C647.** Experiments found intact vegetation reduced maximum tsunami force by up to 70%, but breakage sharply degraded protection and made patch length important. *Regime: Intact vegetation reduced maximum force by up to 70% (rigid) and 66.5% (flexible); breakage reduced protection, although longer patches partly compensated..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Ranasinghege Nipuni Udarika 2025, [doi:10.3390/geosciences15090339](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences15090339))
- **C1396.** Bridge analysis links tsunami-wave impact to connection forces in open-girder coastal bridges, showing that hydrodynamic loading must be transferred through explicit structural connections. *Regime: Deciphering the Tsunami Wave Impact and Associated Connection Forces in Open-Girder Coastal Bridges.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Denis Istrati 2018, [doi:10.3390/jmse6040148](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse6040148))
- **C1708.** Large-scale flume tests indicate that nonbreaking very-long-wave inundation at vertical seawalls produces little impulsive loading: periods of at least 40 s did not exceed about 1.2 times hydrostatic force, unlike bore-led cases. *Regime: Nonbreaking crest- and trough-led very long waves inundating vertical seawalls in a large flume..* [direct_finding, experimental] (David J. McGovern 2022, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2022.104222](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2022.104222))
