## Synthesis

**Well established.** All three reviewed studies reject unbounded extrapolation of moderate-wind drag growth into hurricane winds: field profiles indicate decline, flume measurements indicate a plateau, and surge assimilation favors a peak-and-decline curve. Wind stress is the principal direct momentum forcing for storm surge, but the drag coefficient does not increase without bound at tropical-cyclone winds; observations, experiments and coupled models instead support leveling, decline or strong sea-state modulation.

**Governing physics.** Atmospheric shear transfers momentum through turbulent and wave-supported stress. Roughness, wave age and direction, breaking and whitecaps, foam and spray, currents, stability, fetch, shoaling and land cover modify the magnitude and direction delivered to the water column.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include C_D, Charnock parameter, z0, u_star/U10, wave age c_p/u_star, inverse wave age, wind–wave and wind–stress misalignment angles, relative water depth, fetch, stability, drag saturation wind speed, and nondimensional setup or surge sensitivity.

**Major equations.** The common bulk forcing relation is tau=rho_a Cd U10^2. Peng and Li parameterize high-wind drag as Cd=-a(Vp-33)^2+c, explicitly imposing a peak at 33 m/s. The bulk vector stress is tau=rho_a C_D |U10| U10, with u_star=sqrt(|tau|/rho_a) and logarithmic wind profiles linked to roughness z0. Charnock-type, capped, peak-and-decline, wave-age and coupled spectral closures prescribe C_D or z0 under different regimes.

**Typical methods.** Methods estimate flux from towers, buoys, floats or eddy correlation; infer roughness and whitecaps remotely; reproduce interface behavior in laboratories; compute wave-supported stress spectrally; calibrate drag against setup or surge; and compare closures in coupled forecast ensembles.

**Numerical models.** Reviewed tools include coupled wave–boundary-layer and ocean–wave models, ADCIRC, COAWST/ROMS/SWAN, unstructured tide–surge–wave models, global ocean surge systems, WRF hurricane ensembles and data-assimilative drag calibration.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes coastal-tower typhoon winds to about 42 m/s, surf-zone eddy-correlation profiles, currents beneath five tropical cyclones, microwave radiometry, ten-hurricane coupled hindcasts, Lake Erie setdown and surge events, and regional gauge, buoy, platform, satellite and altimeter comparisons.

**Validated ranges.** The evidence spans distinct regimes: Powell's deep-water eyewall profiles have near-surface winds of 21-67 m/s; Donelan's tank reaches hurricane-equivalent neutral winds above 33 m/s; Peng and Li optimize over reconstructed winds of 10-70 m/s and validate on eight independent cyclones. The evidence spans moderate winds through roughly 55 m/s in combined float/model estimates and about 42 m/s in coastal towers, with some remote or hurricane-model applications higher; transfer remains conditional on measurement method, sea state, depth, storm quadrant and coast.

**Recent advances.** Recent work combines autonomous current profilers, microwave roughness and whitecap retrieval, vector stress estimates, coupled ocean–wave models, wave-dependent surge forcing, large parameter ensembles and regional calibration to replace a universal scalar drag curve.

**Disagreements.** The high-wind asymptote is method dependent. Powell et al. infer a marked decline in open-ocean Cd through U10=51 m/s; Donelan et al. measure saturation at Cd=0.0025 above 33 m/s in a short-fetch flume; Peng and Li fit a regional parabolic decline whose magnitude is slightly higher than Powell and Jarosz at medium-to-high winds. High-wind behavior remains method and regime dependent: some evidence favors saturation, some decline after a peak, and coastal shoaling can shift and raise drag relative to deep water. Scalar wind-only laws also conflict with observed directional and sea-state dependence.

**Limitations.** No reviewed law is universal: Powell explicitly excludes shallow coastal shoaling conditions, Donelan uses short-fetch laboratory waves and extrapolated 10-m winds, and Peng-Li coefficients are regionally inferred and require recalibration elsewhere. Extreme direct stress observations are sparse, winds and waves evolve together, flux and 10 m wind retrievals carry correlated error, laboratory fetch is limited, coastal flow violates open-ocean assumptions, models share closures, and surge calibration can compensate errors in wind, bathymetry or friction.

**Open questions.** Resolving plateau versus decline requires colocated extreme-wind stress, wave-state, foam/spray, current, and shallow-water observations, plus controlled propagation of drag uncertainty into surge forecasts. Priorities are vector stress under wind–wave misalignment, foam and spray physics, current and stability effects, shallow-water transformation, land-cover transitions, storm-quadrant dependence, coupled closure uncertainty, and real-time identification from colocated observations.

**Seminal papers.** Bulk aerodynamic stress, logarithmic boundary-layer theory and Charnock roughness established the classical framework; later field hurricane profiles and high-wind laboratory studies overturned simple extrapolation and motivated capped, declining and sea-state-dependent drag laws.

## Equations

### Quadratic air-sea stress

$$
\tau_s=\rho_a C_D U_{10}^2
$$

Regime: ADCIRC hurricane wind forcing with the reported Garratt relation and drag cap.

Variables: `tau_s` surface wind stress; `rho_a` air density; `C_D` air-sea drag coefficient; `U10` 10 m wind speed

Source: (Bunya 2010, [doi:10.1175/2009mwr2906.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/2009mwr2906.1))

## Claims

- **C41.** In 331 tropical-cyclone eyewall profiles, friction velocity increased with U10 to about 40 m/s and then leveled off, while neutral drag showed a decreasing tendency above about 33 m/s. *Regime: Deep-water open-ocean eyewalls; near-surface winds 21-67 m/s..* [direct_finding, field] (Powell 2003, [doi:10.1038/nature01481](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01481))
- **C42.** The inferred roughness length and drag coefficient decreased sharply as U10 increased to 51 m/s and were much lower than Charnock and Large-Pond extrapolations. *Regime: Resolved open-ocean wind groups through U10=51 m/s; not the under-sampled 70-85 m/s MBL group..* [direct_finding, field] (Powell 2003, [doi:10.1038/nature01481](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01481))
- **C43.** The mean 10-m wind was 78% of mean boundary-layer wind, with an 8% standard deviation. *Regime: The paper's deep-water eyewall sample..* [direct_finding, field] (Powell 2003, [doi:10.1038/nature01481](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01481))
- **C44.** Applying the reported open-ocean reduction factors near shore is not justified because shoaling-wave studies indicate higher coastal roughness. *Regime: Coastal shallow water and hurricane landfall..* [direct_finding, review] (Powell 2003, [doi:10.1038/nature01481](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01481))
- **C45.** Laboratory drag increased between 3 and 33 m/s but saturated once neutral 10-m wind exceeded 33 m/s. *Regime: University of Miami wind-wave tank and its short-fetch waves..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Donelan 2004, [doi:10.1029/2004gl019460](https://doi.org/10.1029/2004gl019460))
- **C46.** The measured high-wind saturation level was Cd=0.0025, corresponding to aerodynamic roughness length z0=3.35 mm. *Regime: Neutral 10-m-equivalent wind above 33 m/s in the flume..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Donelan 2004, [doi:10.1029/2004gl019460](https://doi.org/10.1029/2004gl019460))
- **C47.** Continuous breaking can limit aerodynamic roughness because separated outer airflow skips from crest to crest and does not follow wave troughs. *Regime: Steep spilling breakers under extreme wind..* [inferred_relationship, experimental] (Donelan 2004, [doi:10.1029/2004gl019460](https://doi.org/10.1029/2004gl019460))
- **C48.** For 18 South China Sea tropical cyclones, the study represented drag as Cd=-a(Vp-33)^2+c, with ten cases for calibration and eight for independent validation. *Regime: South China Sea cyclones during 2006-2011 with modeled winds 10-70 m/s..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Peng 2015, [doi:10.1038/srep15496](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep15496))
- **C49.** Across the eight validation cyclones, the optimized parabolic drag law produced maximum-surge-bias standard deviation 0.117 m and mean RMSE 0.109 m, the lowest aggregate pair in Table 2. *Regime: The paper's eight South China Sea validation cyclones and gauge/model configuration..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Peng 2015, [doi:10.1038/srep15496](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep15496))
- **C50.** The fitted South China Sea curve had the same peak-and-decline trend as Powell and Jarosz but slightly larger drag throughout the medium-to-high-wind band. *Regime: The reconstructed regional winds and assimilated surge cases..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Peng 2015, [doi:10.1038/srep15496](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep15496))
- **C51.** The fitted coefficients are not asserted to transfer unchanged outside the South China Sea and should be recalibrated with regional observations. *Regime: Application to other coastal regions..* [direct_finding, review] (Peng 2015, [doi:10.1038/srep15496](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep15496))
- **C1078.** A coupled wave–wind parameterization derived from ten Atlantic tropical cyclones makes roughness increase while high-wind drag levels off, improving hurricane-model intensity behavior. *Regime: A Physics-Based Parameterization of Air–Sea Momentum Flux at High Wind Speeds and Its Impact on Hurricane Intensity Predictions.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Il‐Ju Moon 2007, [doi:10.1175/mwr3432.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/mwr3432.1))
- **C1079.** Coupled wave-boundary-layer experiments show that tropical-cyclone drag is controlled by the spatially varying wave field, with developed right-front seas producing higher drag than young rear-left seas. *Regime: Effect of Surface Waves on Air–Sea Momentum Exchange. Part II: Behavior of Drag Coefficient under Tropical Cyclones.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Il‐Ju Moon 2004, [doi:10.1175/1520-0469(2004)061<2334:eoswoa>2.0.co;2](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0469(2004)061<2334:eoswoa>2.0.co;2))
- **C1080.** Laboratory and numerical evidence attributes hurricane-force interface structure and stress behavior to Kelvin–Helmholtz-like shear instability and associated air–water interfacial change. *Regime: The air-sea interface and surface stress under tropical cyclones.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Alexander Soloviev 2014, [doi:10.1038/srep05306](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep05306))
- **C1081.** A storm-surge modeling comparison demonstrates that alternative wind-stress conversion and drag formulations materially change simulated surge and require regime-aware selection. *Regime: An Exploration of Wind Stress Calculation Techniques in Hurricane Storm Surge Modeling.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Kyra Bryant 2016, [doi:10.3390/jmse4030058](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse4030058))
- **C1082.** Coastal-tower measurements to about 42 m/s show a drag peak near 24 m/s and a curve shifted from open-ocean behavior, consistent with shoaling-wave effects during typhoon landfall. *Regime: Typhoon air‐sea drag coefficient in coastal regions.* [direct_finding, field] (Zhongkuo Zhao 2015, [doi:10.1002/2014jc010283](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014jc010283))
- **C1083.** Hurricane Ike SWAN experiments show that wind-input and maximum-drag choices control high-wind wave estimates and can correct a roughly 2 m deep-Gulf wave-height overestimate. *Regime: Gulf of Mexico hurricane wave simulations using SWAN: Bulk formula‐based drag coefficient sensitivity for Hurricane Ike.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Yong Huang 2013, [doi:10.1002/jgrc.20283](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgrc.20283))
- **C1084.** A coupled wave–wind analysis of ten hurricanes finds high-wind drag leveling and strong dependence of the Charnock coefficient on wave age and storm-relative wave state. *Regime: Effect of surface waves on Charnock coefficient under tropical cyclones.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Il‐Ju Moon 2004, [doi:10.1029/2004gl020988](https://doi.org/10.1029/2004gl020988))
- **C1085.** North Atlantic storm evaluation against buoy, platform, and satellite observations compares five stress parameterizations and shows strong-wind forecasts depend on their high-wind behavior. *Regime: Strong winds in a coupled wave–atmosphere model during a North Atlantic storm event: evaluation against observations.* [direct_finding, field] (Lucia Pineau‐Guillou 2017, [doi:10.1002/qj.3205](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3205))
- **C1086.** Hundreds of convection-permitting Katrina simulations quantify tropical-cyclone sensitivity and parameter uncertainty associated with momentum and enthalpy exchange coefficients. *Regime: Sensitivity of Tropical Cyclone Simulations to Parametric Uncertainties in Air–Sea Fluxes and Implications for Parameter Estimation.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Benjamin W. Green 2014, [doi:10.1175/mwr-d-13-00208.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/mwr-d-13-00208.1))
- **C1087.** A coupled tide–surge–wave model for northeastern Taiwan applies typhoon pressure and wind stress on an unstructured grid to reproduce storm tides and wind waves. *Regime: Simulation of Typhoon-Induced Storm Tides and Wind Waves for the Northeastern Coast of Taiwan Using a Tide–Surge–Wave Coupled Model.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Wei-Bo Chen 2017, [doi:10.3390/w9070549](https://doi.org/10.3390/w9070549))
- **C1088.** ADCIRC experiments show that land-cover specification affects both wind-momentum transfer and bottom dissipation, producing uncertainty in Texas-bay hurricane surge. *Regime: Uncertainty in hurricane surge simulation due to land cover specification.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Celso M. Ferreira 2014, [doi:10.1002/2013jc009604](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013jc009604))
- **C1089.** Current profiles beneath five tropical cyclones yield drag of 0.8–3.1×10⁻³ at 25–40 m/s and stress directions from 15° counterclockwise to 40° clockwise of the wind. *Regime: Scaling of Drag Coefficients Under Five Tropical Cyclones.* [direct_finding, field] (Je‐Yuan Hsu 2019, [doi:10.1029/2018gl081574](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018gl081574))
- **C1090.** Direct eddy-correlation measurements over an open-ocean surf zone find a constant-stress layer and systematic drag changes with wind approach angle and wave conditions. *Regime: Direct measurements of wind stress over the surf zone.* [direct_finding, field] (Behnam Shabani 2014, [doi:10.1002/2013jc009585](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013jc009585))
- **C1091.** North Sea surge cases evaluated with gauges and altimetry show that wave-dependent stress can alter simulated surges relative to wind-only stress. *Regime: Impact of wave-dependent stress on storm surge simulations in the North Sea: Ocean model evaluation against in situ and satellite observations.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Lucia Pineau‐Guillou 2020, [doi:10.1016/j.ocemod.2020.101694](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ocemod.2020.101694))
- **C1092.** Microwave radiometer observations jointly constrain tropical-cyclone high-wind drag, roughness, and whitecap coverage rather than relying solely on extrapolated moderate-wind laws. *Regime: High-Wind Drag Coefficient and Whitecap Coverage Derived from Microwave Radiometer Observations in Tropical Cyclones.* [direct_finding, field] (Hwang 2018, [doi:10.1175/jpo-d-18-0107.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/jpo-d-18-0107.1))
- **C1093.** Float observations and a coupled ocean–wave model give mean drag near 2–3×10⁻³ for 25–55 m/s winds and reveal stronger sea-state than wind-speed dependence, especially under wind–wave misalignment. *Regime: Drag Coefficient and Its Sea State Dependence under Tropical Cyclones.* [direct_finding, field] (Xiaohui Zhou 2022, [doi:10.1175/jpo-d-21-0246.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/jpo-d-21-0246.1))
- **C1094.** Two Lake Erie wind setdown and surge events calibrate air–sea drag with COAWST/ROMS/SWAN, using the upwind shore to reduce contamination by wave setup. *Regime: Using Wind Setdown and Storm Surge on Lake Erie to Calibrate the Air-Sea Drag Coefficient.* [direct_finding, field] (Carl Drews 2013, [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0072510](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0072510))
- **C1776.** C-band SFMR observations from 370 tropical-cyclone missions support high-wind power-law exponents above about 35 m/s of approximately -1 for the 10-m drag coefficient, 0.5 for friction velocity, and 1.25 for whitecap coverage, implying drag saturation and decline rather than continued linear growth. *Regime: Open-ocean tropical-cyclone winds measured by 4.74-7.09 GHz stepped-frequency microwave radiometers, extending beyond 70 m/s, with sea-surface roughness and foam contributions separated through polarimetric thermal-emission modeling..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Hwang 2018, [doi:10.1175/jpo-d-18-0107.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/jpo-d-18-0107.1))
