# Breaking-induced turbulence

`waves.transformation.breaking.turbulence` | Turbulence generated by breaking.

Parent: [Waves and wave transformation](waves.md) > [Wave transformation](waves.transformation.md) > [Breaking](waves.transformation.breaking.md)

Papers: 16. Claims: 15. Equations: 0.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Surface-wave breaking injects vorticity and turbulent kinetic energy through crest overturning, jet impact, rollers and shear; turbulence is intermittent, anisotropic and transported away from the breaker before dissipating or reaching the bed.

**Governing physics.** Plunging jets create impact shear and coherent vortices; spilling rollers sustain near-surface production; vortex stretching and transport redistribute TKE; directional spread changes cascade structure; density/air entrainment modifies buoyancy and pressure; bottom interaction converts advected TKE into stress and suspension.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include breaker type and Iribarren number, wave steepness and relative depth, turbulent Reynolds number, wave period/eddy turnover, roller area/wave height, directional spread, breaker distance/height, TKE/celerity², dissipation normalized by wave-energy flux, and bed distance/roughness.

**Major equations.** Analysis uses Reynolds-decomposed momentum, TKE and Reynolds-stress budgets, vorticity transport, production by mean shear and wave motion, turbulent and pressure transport, buoyancy and dissipation, Kolmogorov/inverse-cascade spectra, LES subgrid closure, RANS k–ω/SST, roller energy, and breaking-probability loss.

**Typical methods.** Methods include high-speed imaging, PIV and ADV, acoustic concentration/velocity, pressure and void fraction, fixed-bar and plane-beach flumes, focused-wave generation, directional basins, phase- and ensemble-averaged TKE budgets, coherent-structure identification, spectra, LES, VOF-RANS and analytical closures.

**Numerical models.** Models include LES of focused breakers, RANS closure comparisons around structures, coherent-vortex simulations of sediment pickup, k–ω/SST and buoyancy modifications represented in uncertain full-text records, spectral cascade analysis, and analytical random-breaking turbulence/dissipation approaches.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes deep-water breaking, plunging-wave flumes, large-scale fixed-bar near-bed TKE budgets, laboratory surf zones with varied directional spread, inner-surf-zone spectra, focused breakers across steepness, and breaking past a vertical cylinder.

**Validated ranges.** The branch covers spilling, plunging, deep-water, focused and inner-surf-zone breaking; crest-to-bed transport; fixed bars and vertical cylinders; narrow to directional wave fields; near-field coherent structures through spectral dissipation; numeric ranges remain experiment-specific.

**Recent advances.** Recent work identifies coherent vortical structures quantitatively, performs steepness-controlled LES, resolves directional-spread cascades, measures coupled energy/dissipation spectra, evaluates closures around structures, and links random breaking occurrence to wave-generated turbulence.

**Disagreements.** RANS closures that reproduce mean wave transformation may disagree on TKE and loads; turbulence can appear locally two-dimensional with inverse cascade while breaker vortices are intrinsically three-dimensional; bottom stress may be driven by advected vortices rather than local orbital shear; dissipation and turbulence are related but not interchangeable.

**Limitations.** Aerated rollers obstruct optical/acoustic measurement; dissipation inference is sensitive to resolution and isotropy assumptions; phase averaging erases intermittency; fixed beds omit feedback; 2-D flumes constrain vortices; LES is expensive and RANS closure constants are not universal.

**Open questions.** Needs include air/bubble energetics, three-dimensional coherent structures, wave-group and current effects, breaker-to-bed pathways, movable-bed feedback, closure under directionality, spectral non-equilibrium, breaking probability, structure/vegetation interaction, and field-scale budget closure.

**Seminal papers.** The branch progresses from plunging-breaker turbulence measurements and deep-water vortex generation to near-bed TKE budgets, coherent-structure sediment suspension, focused-wave LES, directional inverse cascades, inner-surf spectra, and analytical random-breaking closures.

## Claims

- **C828.** RANS turbulence closures differ materially in their ability to reproduce breaking-wave kinematics, loads, and wake turbulence around a vertical cylinder. *Regime: An evaluation of different RANS turbulence models for simulating breaking waves past a vertical cylinder.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Sen Qu 2021, [doi:10.1016/j.oceaneng.2021.109195](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2021.109195))
- **C829.** Breaking waves organize turbulence into coherent vortical structures whose geometry and evolution control transport away from the breaking crest. *Regime: On coherent vortical structures in wave breaking.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Simone Di Giorgio 2022, [doi:10.1017/jfm.2022.674](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2022.674))
- **C830.** Deep-water breaking generates coherent vortices through crest overturning and impact, providing a mechanism for rapid momentum and vorticity injection. *Regime: Vortex generation by deep-water breaking waves.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Nick Pizzo 2013, [doi:10.1017/jfm.2013.453](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2013.453))
- **C831.** Surf-zone breaking turbulence controls suspended-sediment pickup and transport, but measurement and closure differences leave large uncertainty in near-bed TKE. *Regime: Surf Zone Turbulence and Suspended Sediment Dynamics—A Review.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Troels Aagaard 2021, [doi:10.3390/jmse9111300](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse9111300))
- **C832.** Breaking-generated coherent vortices can reach the bed and trigger episodic sediment suspension beyond that predicted by local mean shear alone. *Regime: Vortex-induced suspension of sediment in the surf zone.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Junichi Otsuka 2017, [doi:10.1016/j.advwatres.2017.08.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.advwatres.2017.08.021))
- **C833.** A large-scale plunging breaker produces a strongly phase-dependent near-bed TKE budget over a fixed bar, with transport from the breaking region into the boundary layer. *Regime: Near‐Bed Turbulent Kinetic Energy Budget Under a Large‐Scale Plunging Breaking Wave Over a Fixed Bar.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Joep van der Zanden 2018, [doi:10.1002/2017jc013411](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017jc013411))
- **C834.** Large-eddy simulations show focused-breaker turbulence and dissipation vary systematically with incident wave steepness. *Regime: Large eddy simulation of focused breaking waves with different wave steepness.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Ting Cui 2022, [doi:10.1016/j.ocemod.2022.102122](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ocemod.2022.102122))
- **C835.** Laboratory surf-zone turbulence exhibits a two-dimensional inverse energy cascade whose strength changes with directional wave spread. *Regime: Two-dimensional inverse energy cascade in a laboratory surf zone for varying wave directional spread.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Christine M. Baker 2023, [doi:10.1063/5.0169895](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0169895))
- **C836.** Inner-surf-zone measurements resolve coupled wave-energy and dissipation spectra, exposing scale-dependent transfer through breaking turbulence. *Regime: Energy and dissipation spectra of waves propagating in the inner surf zone.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Philippe Bonneton 2023, [doi:10.1017/jfm.2023.878](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2023.878))
- **C837.** Measurements within a plunging breaker identify localized, rapidly evolving turbulence associated with jet impact and roller motion. *Regime: INVESTIGATION OF TURBULENCE IN A PLUNGING BREAKING WAVE.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Toomas Liiv 2001, [doi:10.3176/eng.2001.1.06](https://doi.org/10.3176/eng.2001.1.06))
- **C838.** Analytical closures link wave-generated turbulence and random breaking occurrence to wave-energy dissipation while making their regime assumptions explicit. *Regime: Analytical approaches for wave energy dissipation induced by wave-generated turbulence and random wave-breaking.* [direct_finding, analytical] (Yang 2026, [doi:10.5194/os-22-1587-2026](https://doi.org/10.5194/os-22-1587-2026))
- **C1676.** Breaking-bore rollers contain spatially partitioned, short-lived air-water features whose geometry, frequency and surface velocity link free-surface structure to energy dissipation and characteristic time/length scales. *Regime: Unsteady laboratory breaking bores and roller free surfaces across the tested Froude-number conditions..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Davide Wüthrich 2021, [doi:10.1017/jfm.2021.614](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2021.614))
- **C1755.** For spilling and plunging regular breakers, buoyancy-modified k-omega closures preserve laminar-like low turbulence during pre-breaking propagation while recovering fully turbulent surf-zone flow, reducing the common RANS overprediction of turbulent kinetic energy relative to the unmodified k-omega SST model. *Regime: Regular spilling and plunging waves breaking over a fixed sloping bed in a two-phase numerical wave flume corresponding to the Ting and Kirby laboratory cases..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Devolder 2018, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2018.04.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2018.04.011))
- **C1758.** No standard RANS closure is uniformly reliable for spilling and plunging breakers: in OpenFOAM comparisons against surface elevation, undertow, and TKE data, nonlinear k-epsilon gave the best overall accuracy-efficiency compromise, while some closures overpredicted spilling-breaker TKE so strongly that a zero-turbulence calculation was more accurate. *Regime: Two-dimensional regular spilling and plunging breakers over a 1:35 laboratory beach, simulated with two-phase RANS-VOF under Ting and Kirby wave conditions..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Scott Brown 2016, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2016.04.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2016.04.002))
- **C1763.** Large-scale bore-driven swash carries two distinct air populations—a locally entrained wave-tip cloud and residual plunge-generated bubbles—but aeration decays rapidly during uprush and, despite high local void fractions, contributes only a small share of total swash-zone energy dissipation under the tested condition. *Regime: A single repeatable plunging bore and uprush/backwash event on a laboratory 1:10 impermeable sand-rough slope..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Han-Jing Dai 2016, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2016.10.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2016.10.002))
