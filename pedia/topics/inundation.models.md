## Synthesis

**Well established.** Coastal inundation prediction requires explicit wetting and drying plus defensible marine and terrestrial forcing. Validation must be process- and site-specific: agreement for one tsunami geometry, hurricane, city, resolution, or water-level metric does not establish universal model skill.

**Governing physics.** The reviewed models combine long-wave or reduced shallow-water propagation, surge, wave setup and runup, rainfall-runoff and river inflow, bed and building drag, topographic control, urban blockage, and moving shorelines. Which mechanisms are retained materially changes predicted extent and depth.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Important nondimensional controls include relative tsunami amplitude H/d, Froude and friction measures, relative terrain and building scale, ensemble exceedance probability, normalized error metrics, and ratios of wave, surge, tide, rainfall, and river contributions to total water level.

**Major equations.** Core formulations include nonlinear Boussinesq or shallow-water continuity and momentum equations, bottom-friction and directional building-drag source terms, wet-dry algorithms, nonstationary extreme-value and joint-probability models, raster depth-damage functions, and ensemble exceedance probabilities.

**Typical methods.** Typical methods couple terrain and bathymetry preprocessing with depth-averaged or reduced-complexity solvers, deterministic hindcasts, probabilistic forcing ensembles, laboratory or gage validation, observed-extent comparison, sensitivity analysis, and GIS exposure or damage overlays.

**Numerical models.** Reviewed models include a higher-order Boussinesq inundation model, coupled MIKE 21 and XBeach configurations, an RDM plus Zero-Point Boundary method, a distributed hydrologic-GIS damage chain, an urban Drag Force Model, SFINCS compound-flood ensembles, and explicit high-precision 3D urban simulation.

**Experimental datasets.** Validation evidence includes Synolakis plane-beach and Briggs conical-island runup tests, the 1:250 HyTOFU Onagawa urban-tsunami experiment, NOAA and USGS Hurricane Ike records, Colombian Hurricane Iota profiles and flood maps, surveyed Haiyan inundation limits, and observed Korean coastal-city flood extent.

**Validated ranges.** Reported validation regimes span solitary-wave plane beaches and conical islands with H/d of 0.10-0.20, a 1:250 idealized Onagawa city, Hurricane Iota in the Colombian archipelago, a 5 m Haiyan terrain model with 81% surveyed-limit accuracy, and Hurricane Ike ensembles of 81-1000 members at one- to three-day lead times.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances pair reduced-complexity coastal solvers with large forcing ensembles, retain useful multi-day forecast skill, parameterize unresolved buildings directionally, resolve cities with LiDAR and drone-derived geometry, and report omission warnings when computational constraints remove wave effects.

**Disagreements.** The evidence does not support one universally preferred representation of urban form: explicit high-resolution geometry can improve routing and visualization, while subgrid directional drag can capture building effects more efficiently. Their relative accuracy and cost remain configuration-dependent.

**Limitations.** Common limitations are uncertain topography and bathymetry, forcing and dependence assumptions, friction and building closure, omitted waves or compound drivers, limited field observations, resolution sensitivity, computational cost, and validation against only one or a few historical events.

**Open questions.** Priorities include transferable urban roughness and blockage closures, multi-event and multi-site benchmarks, explicit wave-pluvial-fluvial-surge coupling, nonstationary ensemble generation, terrain and friction uncertainty propagation, probabilistic spatial validation, and operational accuracy-cost criteria.

**Seminal papers.** Within this screened branch, the 2015 higher-order Boussinesq application established explicit regional tsunami inundation with laboratory runup validation, while the 2017 Manhattan study linked nonstationary compound forcing to mapped damage.

## Claims

- **C218.** For Manhattan, incorporating nonstationarity increased modeled coastal floodplain extent and damage, whereas defining scenarios with the joint probability of rainfall and water level reduced them relative to combinations that did not account for dependence. *Regime: Manhattan rainfall and coastal water-level records, the study's three scenarios, distributed model, DEM, land-use, and depth-damage functions..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Karamouz 2017, [doi:10.1061/(asce)ir.1943-4774.0001173](https://doi.org/10.1061/(asce)ir.1943-4774.0001173))
- **C219.** A drag-force inundation model represented unresolved urban structures with characteristic height and directional projected areas and was validated against the 1:250 HyTOFU idealization of Onagawa. *Regime: Tsunami inundation through building fields representable by the three DFM parameters; validation is bounded by the 1:250 idealized Onagawa experiment..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Fukui 2019, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2019.103534](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2019.103534))
- **C220.** For Hurricane Ike on the Texas Gulf Coast, SFINCS ensemble means reduced average station errors by 16% versus a deterministic case, and a roughly 100-member ensemble ran in under four hours on one CPU while retaining useful one- to three-day flood guidance. *Regime: Hurricane Ike, the modeled Texas Gulf Coast domain, 81-1000 members, one- to three-day lead times, stated forcing and terrain data, and SFINCS assumptions..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Lee 2025, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2025.104721](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2025.104721))
- **C221.** In a Korean coastal-city case, a high-precision 3D urban inundation model reasonably matched observed flood extent and indicated that explicit building geometry matters for routing and visualization, although quantitative skill was not reported in the accessible record. *Regime: The studied Korean coastal city and the available high-definition maps, LiDAR, drone imagery, and 3D city representation..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Kim 2021, [doi:10.2112/jcr-si114-092.1](https://doi.org/10.2112/jcr-si114-092.1))
- **C1166.** A coupled rainfall–runoff–inundation model using 2D diffusion-wave equations reproduced the MODIS-derived large-scale 2010 Kabul River flood extent and identified flash-flood damage areas across a 92,605 km² basin. *Regime: Rainfall–runoff–inundation analysis of the 2010 Pakistan flood in the Kabul River basin.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Takahiro Sayama 2012, [doi:10.1080/02626667.2011.644245](https://doi.org/10.1080/02626667.2011.644245))
- **C1167.** Historical dam-break validation data are most useful when breach geometry, maximum depths, arrival times, water marks, flood extent, topography and scenario inputs are sufficiently documented; the review separates complete from partial test cases. *Regime: Review of Historical Dam-Break Events and Laboratory Tests on Real Topography for the Validation of Numerical Models.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Francesca Aureli 2021, [doi:10.3390/w13141968](https://doi.org/10.3390/w13141968))
- **C1169.** For ungauged Hurghada, an integrated GIS, remote-sensing, 2D rainfall–runoff–inundation and multicriteria workflow mapped hazard and seven-component vulnerability for 5-, 10- and 50-year return periods. *Regime: Integrated Methodology for Urban Flood Risk Mapping at the Microscale in Ungauged Regions: A Case Study of Hurghada, Egypt.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Karim I. Abdrabo 2020, [doi:10.3390/rs12213548](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12213548))
- **C1170.** The low-fidelity/spatial-analysis/Gaussian-process surrogate predicted both flood depth and extent on an unstructured Chowilla floodplain grid while running 12 times faster than the high-resolution hydrodynamic model. *Regime: Development of a Fast and Accurate Hybrid Model for Floodplain Inundation Simulations.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Niels Fraehr 2023, [doi:10.1029/2022wr033836](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022wr033836))
- **C1174.** Cork scenarios show current extreme inundation is primarily fluvial, high-end future coastal inundation can become comparable, and the largest city-centre floods arise from combined river discharge and coastal water level. *Regime: Modelling the effects of climate change on urban coastal-fluvial flooding.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Jennifer Isabel Munro Kirkpatrick 2020, [doi:10.2166/wcc.2020.166](https://doi.org/10.2166/wcc.2020.166))
- **C1175.** For the 2000 Langat River event, satellite-derived quantitative-precipitation forecasts drove pre-flood MIKE11 GIS inundation maps with 70% agreement at sampled observed flood points. *Regime: Pre‐flood inundation mapping for flood early warning.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Billa 2011, [doi:10.1111/j.1753-318x.2011.01115.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1753-318x.2011.01115.x))
- **C1450.** A dual-grid shallow-water framework enables fine-resolution metropolitan inundation forecasts and is evaluated against Hurricane Harvey observations. *Regime: A Framework for Mechanistic Flood Inundation Forecasting at the Metropolitan Scale.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Jochen E. Schubert 2022, [doi:10.1029/2021wr031279](https://doi.org/10.1029/2021wr031279))
- **C1614.** A coastal-flood modeling review finds that static bathtub methods perform poorly in defended urban backshores and that credible prediction requires resolved terrain and infrastructure, time-varying overtopping, overland routing, hydrologic inputs, and interacting compound pathways. *Regime: Defended urban coastal backshores exposed to marine, overtopping, hydrologic, groundwater and infrastructure-mediated flooding..* [literature_review_statement, review] (T. W. Gallien 2018, [doi:10.3390/geosciences8120450](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences8120450))
- **C1759.** Parcel-scale urban coastal flood prediction requires dynamic coupling of wave overtopping, overland shallow-water flow, defenses, and storm drainage: static bathtub mapping substantially overpredicts transient flooding, while walls can paradoxically worsen overtopping floods by restricting drainage and temporary beach berms can sharply reduce inundation. *Regime: Short-duration wave-overtopping flooding of the densely urbanized Balboa Peninsula at Newport Beach, California, with bay-side walls, storm drains, streets, and temporary sand berms..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Gallien 2014, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2014.04.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2014.04.007))
