# Hybrid defenses

`nature_based.hybrid` | Combined natural and gray infrastructure.

Parent: [Nature-based coastal protection](nature_based.md)

Papers: 27. Claims: 26. Equations: 2.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Hybrid defense can reduce flood and erosion risk by placing a natural or nature-based feature seaward of, beside, or within engineered infrastructure, but performance depends on forcing, feature geometry and condition, available space, structure configuration, ecological persistence, maintenance, and governance. Combining components does not guarantee synergy.

**Governing physics.** Green components attenuate waves through breaking, drag and friction, store or route flood water, and influence sediment; gray components resist residual overtopping, impact and erosion. Dike blockage, marsh and channel geometry, platform inundation, vegetation deformation or breakage, wave steepness, freeboard, and component ordering determine transmitted load and structural response.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Important nondimensional controls include relative water depth and freeboard, marsh-platform water fraction, relative width and channel depth, wave steepness, vegetation submergence and density, drag and transmission coefficients, proportional overtopping reduction, failure probability, benefit-cost ratios, and intuitionistic membership, non-membership and hesitancy.

**Major equations.** Core formulations include depth-averaged marsh hydrodynamics, platform-to-total water-volume control, wave-energy and vegetation-drag attenuation, overtopping discharge, revetment impact and fragility functions, series-component reduction Rh=Rg+Rr-RgRr, structural-response equations forced by transmitted waves, reliability integrals, and fuzzy multi-criteria distance-to-ideal rankings.

**Typical methods.** Evidence combines calibrated two-dimensional hydrodynamic scenarios, stochastic reliability analysis, large-scale physical models, analytical coupling of attenuation and structural response, ecosystem and case reviews, stakeholder interviews, lifecycle and co-benefit assessment, expert elicitation, and multi-criteria decision analysis.

**Numerical models.** Models include TELEMAC2D for marsh-dike propagation, stochastic foreshore-dike reliability for overtopping and revetment impact, empirical series-component overtopping reduction, coupled attenuation-structure equations, blue-carbon and multifunctionality frameworks, and Fuzzy Delphi plus entropy-weighted IF-TOPSIS selection.

**Experimental datasets.** Key evidence includes water levels across a 3000 ha Dutch marsh and two storm tides, a large-scale wall-mangrove-revetment overtopping campaign, reviewed UK and southeast Asian marsh and mangrove cases, interviews with 27 residents in two New Jersey communities, and Golden Coast expert judgments across 18 measures and 63 initial indicators.

**Validated ranges.** Reported regimes include a 3000 ha marsh needing about 6-10 km width to avoid tested dike-blockage effects, attenuation onset at platform-water fractions 0.2-0.4 and maxima of 29 cm/km near 0.85, a nonbreaking overtopping experiment whose series model was accurate within 5%, 27 interview participants, and 18 consensus indicators selected from 63 to rank 18 measures.

**Recent advances.** Recent work advances from qualitative hybrid advocacy to component-resolved overtopping experiments, coupled green-to-gray design equations, climate-adaptive lifecycle planning, quantified multifunctionality, and fuzzy group decisions that preserve expert uncertainty and stakeholder priorities.

**Disagreements.** The finding that one tested hybrid behaved as independent series components does not establish universal absence of interaction: breaking, sediment feedback, vegetation damage, canopy, different gray geometry, and long-term ecological change were outside that experiment. Preference for natural features alongside perceived superior gray effectiveness also reflects trust and communication, not hydraulic comparison alone.

**Limitations.** Evidence remains sparse across extreme storms, breaking waves, component degradation, sediment evolution, ecological succession, maintenance cycles, and long design lives. Cost comparisons are thin; co-benefit valuation is scale-sensitive; stakeholder samples and expert weights are local; and natural-feature uncertainty is often larger than gray-component uncertainty.

**Open questions.** Priorities are full-scale extreme-event validation, coupled hydro-morphodynamic-ecological reliability, interaction versus series criteria, fragility and maintenance models for living components, adaptive thresholds under sea-level rise, standardized lifecycle cost and co-benefit accounting, and participatory selection that updates as evidence changes.

**Seminal papers.** Within this screened branch, the 2016 marsh-dike study establishes geometry- and blockage-dependent flood attenuation, and the 2018 reliability study translates variable natural protection into explicit dike failure probability.

## Equations

### Series-component overtopping reduction

$$
R_h=R_g+R_r-R_gR_r
$$

Regime: Nonbreaking tests at constant freeboard and depth with the tested mangrove and revetment geometries.

Variables: `R_h` proportional hybrid overtopping reduction; `R_g` proportional green-component reduction; `R_r` proportional revetment reduction

Source: (Libby 2024, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2024.104615](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2024.104615))

### Coupled green-gray design chain

$$
H_t=K_tH_i,\qquad R_s=\mathcal{F}(H_t,T,h,\mathrm{geometry})
$$

Regime: Mangrove-fronted gray infrastructure within the source model assumptions.

Variables: `H_i` incident wave height; `K_t` green-feature transmission or attenuation factor; `H_t` wave height reaching gray structure; `R_s` structural response or design demand; `F` structure-response relation

Source: (Libby 2026, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2025.104877](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2025.104877))
