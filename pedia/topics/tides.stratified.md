# Stratified coastal circulation

`tides.stratified` | Density-influenced circulation.

Parent: [Tides and coastal circulation](tides.md)

Papers: 21. Claims: 20. Equations: 0.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Density stratification converts barotropic tidal forcing into baroclinic circulation, internal tides, fronts and phase-dependent turbulence; mixing is intermittent and localized by topography, pycnocline structure, lateral gradients, tidal phase and wind.

**Governing physics.** Tidal flow over topography generates internal waves and beams; shoaling steepens them into bores and shear instability; tidal straining alternately strengthens and weakens stratification; friction and lateral density gradients rectify oscillations into residual circulation; mixing feeds back on stratification.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include Richardson, Froude, Reynolds and Stokes numbers, tidal excursion/topographic scale, internal-wave frequency/N and Coriolis ratio, pycnocline depth/depth, barotropic speed/internal-wave speed, Simpson–Hunter h/u³, bottom drag, stratification and lateral-gradient strength.

**Major equations.** Frameworks use Boussinesq momentum and density transport, buoyancy frequency N², gradient Richardson number, internal-wave dispersion and energy flux, turbulent kinetic-energy/dissipation budgets, buoyancy flux and mixing efficiency, baroclinic pressure, residual covariance, and Simpson–Hunter or Stokes mixing criteria.

**Typical methods.** Methods include microstructure profilers, ADCP and moored thermistor/salinity chains, dye/tracer and ship sections, PIV and bottom turbulence, internal-wave beam/energy diagnostics, harmonic and residual decomposition, hydrostatic/nonhydrostatic models, idealized channels, and mixing/energy budgets.

**Numerical models.** Models represent internal-tide mode cascade, Arctic tidal mixing, lateral and residual estuarine circulation, Stokes-number water-column regimes, stratified inlet circulation, wind-modulated dissipation, and minor-axis straining that concentrates sediment.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence spans the New England shelf, partially stratified estuaries, continental slopes, barrier reefs, Kaena Ridge, Monterey Bay, East China Sea, a coastal embayment, tidal inlets, periodically stratified channels and the Arctic Ocean.

**Validated ranges.** The evidence covers well-mixed through partially and strongly stratified water columns, bottom boundary layers through pycnoclines, estuary and inlet to shelf/slope and Arctic scales, low- and high-mode internal tides, semidiurnal and spring–neap variability, and wind-upwelling modulation.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances coordinate internal-wave process teams, resolve low-to-high-mode energy cascade, measure straining with microstructure across environments, connect winds to semidiurnal dissipation, and diagnose lateral stratification effects in inlets and freshwater-influenced seas.

**Disagreements.** A water column classified as well mixed in the mean can still undergo tidal straining; mixing is not spatially uniform along beams or slopes; bottom stress alone does not predict dissipation when internal waves shoal; and residual circulation needs lateral as well as vertical stratification asymmetry.

**Limitations.** Microstructure captures limited space/time; mixing efficiency is uncertain; hydrostatic models miss overturning; internal-wave beams interact with rough three-dimensional topography and mesoscale flow; boundary conditions and unresolved turbulence strongly affect energy closure.

**Open questions.** Priorities include complete barotropic-to-baroclinic energy pathways, mode conversion and scattering, wave–mean and wave–mesoscale interaction, lateral/submesoscale fronts, mixing efficiency, sediment/biology feedback, changing climate stratification, and transferable parameterizations.

**Seminal papers.** The branch links estuarine turbulence and periodic stratification to tidal/lateral asymmetry, internal-tide beams and ridge mixing, straining in well-mixed channels, frictional shelf-sea regime maps, and minor-axis sediment convergence.
