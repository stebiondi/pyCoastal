# Rip currents

`nearshore.rips` | Seaward-directed concentrated currents.

Parent: [Nearshore hydrodynamics](nearshore.md)

Papers: 23. Claims: 22. Equations: 0.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Rip currents are concentrated seaward flows in and beyond the surf zone. They may form exiting jets or semi-enclosed circulations and arise from hydrodynamic instability, bathymetric focusing, or lateral boundaries.

**Governing physics.** Breaking-wave setup gradients, pressure gradients, vorticity, advection, vortex force, wave direction and spectrum, water level, bathymetry, and boundary geometry jointly determine rip occurrence, strength, position, and pathway.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Transfer depends on normalized wave height and breaking state, wave angle and spectral partition, relative water level and bar/channel geometry, morphology wavelength relative to surf-zone width, and time or separation scaling of dispersion.

**Major equations.** Reviewed approaches span extended-Boussinesq wave-current equations, depth-averaged momentum and continuity, wave-action and breaking closures, sediment continuity and transport, Lagrangian dispersion measures, and ensemble state estimation.

**Typical methods.** Methods combine process classification, field current meters and drifters, remote sensing, dredged channels, movable-bed laboratories, Boussinesq and wave-averaged circulation models, morphodynamic stability models, COAWST, and ensemble Kalman filtering.

**Numerical models.** Models reproduce laboratory rip instability, bar-channel self-organization, nonlinear merging and migration, breaking and setup gradients in dredged channels, tidal modulation, canyon-controlled intermittency, and bathymetric data assimilation.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes eight movable-bed morphologies, five roughly 30 m wide and 1.5 m deep dredged channels, seven Sand City drifter days, 32 macro-tidal cycles, Duck remote sensing, and month-long canyon-coast observations.

**Validated ranges.** Reported values include morphological wavelengths of 180-250 m linearly and 165-320 m nonlinearly, cross-shore diffusivity initially 5.4-6.1 m2/s, total-to-turbulent dispersion ratios of 3-30, and macro-tidal peaks about 1.5 h around low water.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances use dense drifters, remote-sensing assimilation, controlled dredged channels, time-varying morphodynamic forcing, and month-scale spectral simulations to resolve intermittency, retention, and forecast sensitivity.

**Disagreements.** The traditional persistent exit-flow picture is incomplete: field evidence also shows recirculation and nearshore retention. Regular spacings from stationary models contrast with irregular morphology under time-varying forcing and antecedent-bathymetry sensitivity.

**Limitations.** Prediction is limited by unknown and evolving bathymetry, transient wave spectra and angles, tide, mixed forcing mechanisms, model dimensionality and closures, sparse three-dimensional observations, and uncertain links from flow to human hazard.

**Open questions.** Key needs are operational flash-rip prediction, three-dimensional vertical structure, morphology-aware ensembles, boundary and offshore-feature interactions, swimmer and rescue response, and standardized hazard validation across rip types.

**Seminal papers.** The branch connects early extended-Boussinesq rip-system validation and instability-based bar models to later Lagrangian field studies and the six-type process classification; detailed historical attribution remains a full-text task.

## Claims

- **C421.** Rip currents can be classified as hydrodynamically controlled shear-instability or flash rips, bathymetrically controlled channel or focused rips, and boundary-controlled deflection or shadow rips, while real beaches commonly mix these mechanisms. *Regime: wave-exposed coasts and six rip types.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Bruno Castelle 2016, [doi:10.1016/j.earscirev.2016.09.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2016.09.008))
- **C422.** A fully nonlinear extended-Boussinesq model with localized breaking dissipation and moving shoreline reproduced measured wave height, mean water level, velocity components, and rip-current instability. *Regime: laboratory rip-current system with moving shoreline.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Qin Chen 1999, [doi:10.1029/1999jc900154](https://doi.org/10.1029/1999jc900154))
- **C423.** In the modelled single-barred coast, rip-cell spacing scaled with shore-to-bar crest distance, increased with wave height toward saturation, and responded jointly to wave incidence angle and period. *Regime: straight single-barred sandy coast under normal and near-normal waves.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Daniel Calvete 2005, [doi:10.1029/2004jc002803](https://doi.org/10.1029/2004jc002803))
- **C424.** Modelled feedback between wave-driven currents and sediment-concentration gradients favoured crescentic bars under energetic fine-sediment conditions and transverse bars under milder coarse-sediment conditions. *Regime: initially planar sandy slope under normally incident saturated-surf-zone waves.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Miquel Caballería 2002, [doi:10.1017/s002211200200112x](https://doi.org/10.1017/s002211200200112x))
- **C425.** A nonlinear morphodynamic model reproduced longshore-bar to rhythmic-bar to transverse-bar-and-rip transitions, with wavelengths of 180-250 m in the linear regime and 165-320 m in the nonlinear regime. *Regime: longshore bar evolving through linear and nonlinear regimes.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Roland Garnier 2008, [doi:10.1029/2007jc004632](https://doi.org/10.1029/2007jc004632))
- **C426.** Across eight moveable-bed morphologies under identical waves, rip-current intensity increased linearly with beach alongshore nonuniformity, while neck-flow pulsation and directional variability changed systematically with morphology. *Regime: eight beach morphologies under identical shore-normal waves and water level.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Bruno Castelle 2010, [doi:10.1029/2010jc006343](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010jc006343))
- **C427.** At Duck, assimilating remote-sensing products with an ensemble Kalman filter improved nearshore bathymetry estimates and rip-current forecasts without requiring in-situ observations. *Regime: September 2010 Duck, North Carolina field experiment.* [direct_finding, mixed] (G. Wilson 2014, [doi:10.1002/2013jc009213](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013jc009213))
- **C428.** Rip-channel spacing and growth are inherently sensitive to the initial cross-shore bathymetry at a level comparable to wave-height sensitivity, so predictors must retain antecedent morphology. *Regime: pattern development on barred beaches.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Daniel Calvete 2007, [doi:10.1029/2006gl028162](https://doi.org/10.1029/2006gl028162))
- **C429.** Time-varying wave incidence produced different mean rip spacing, weaker and more irregular morphology, more merging and splitting, and net longshore migration not reproduced by time-invariant forcing. *Regime: rip-channel formation under time-varying versus integrated stationary forcing.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Bruno Castelle 2011, [doi:10.1029/2011jf001997](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011jf001997))
- **C430.** For five dredged surf-zone channels, maximum offshore flow correlated with breaking-driven setup gradients and alongshore flow, increasing with bar dissipation until limited by the vertical scale of bathymetric variability. *Regime: channels about 30 m wide and 1.5 m deep across the surf zone.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Melissa Moulton 2017, [doi:10.1002/2016jc012222](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016jc012222))
- **C431.** At Sand City, rip flow produced initial cross-shore diffusivity of 5.4-6.1 m2/s before declining to 0.9-2.2 m2/s, and total dispersion exceeded small-scale turbulent diffusion by factors of 3-30. *Regime: rip-channeled Sand City beach across waves and tides.* [direct_finding, field] (Jeff Brown 2009, [doi:10.1029/2008jc005158](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008jc005158))
- **C432.** On a rip-channeled beach, drifters exited in rip pulses and returned over shoals, demonstrating wave-driven surf-zone-inner-shelf exchange that retained surface material nearshore and was not due to Stokes drift alone. *Regime: rip-channeled Sand City beach under weak winds.* [direct_finding, field] (Jenna Brown 2015, [doi:10.1175/jpo-d-14-0118.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/jpo-d-14-0118.1))
- **C433.** Across 32 tidal cycles, macro-tidal rip-neck flow peaked about 1.5 hours before and after low water as breaking and alongshore pressure gradients shifted across bar-rip morphology. *Regime: macro-tidal bar-rip beach across wave and tidal conditions.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Martin Austin 2013, [doi:10.1016/j.csr.2013.12.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csr.2013.12.004))
- **C434.** During a month-long canyon-coast simulation, incident energy direction and the energy ratio of bimodal spectra controlled rip-current formation, wave period controlled position, and persistent offshore bathymetry dominated the circulation response. *Regime: Nearshore Canyon Experiment coast with strong offshore bathymetric gradients.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Long 2015, [doi:10.1002/2015jc010990](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015jc010990))
- **C1207.** Scanning sonar observed episodic rip jets recurring roughly one to four times per hour, lasting minutes and reaching about 0.7 m/s while exporting warm, turbid, bubble-rich surf-zone water offshore. *Regime: Observations of nearshore circulation: Rip currents.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Smith 1995, [doi:10.1029/95jc00751](https://doi.org/10.1029/95jc00751))
- **C1209.** On a longshore-uniform swell beach, transient non-topographic rips formed 20–30 m necks and spreading vortex-rich heads; intermittent heads greatly enhanced dispersion outside the surf zone. *Regime: Transient rip currents and nearshore circulation on a swell‐dominated beach.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Johnson 2004, [doi:10.1029/2003jc001798](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003jc001798))
- **C1210.** Dense laboratory drifters showed highly unsteady rip circulation, significant Stokes-drift contributions, circulation generation balanced by bottom-friction dissipation, and biases from depth-varying transport and drifter concentration. *Regime: Drifter measurements in a laboratory rip current.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Andrew B. Kennedy 2004, [doi:10.1029/2003jc001927](https://doi.org/10.1029/2003jc001927))
- **C1211.** A roughly 0.3 m Lake Michigan meteotsunami generated hazardous rip currents on an O(1 m) model grid within minutes of calm conditions and sustained them for hours, identifying a distinct rip-generation mechanism. *Regime: Unexpected rip currents induced by a meteotsunami.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Álvaro Linares 2019, [doi:10.1038/s41598-019-38716-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-38716-2))
- **C1215.** At a headland-and-reef beach, small wave/tide changes shifted three circulation regimes; under 4 m oblique waves a deflection rip reached 0.7 m/s 800 m offshore in 12 m depth and could export sediment beyond closure depth. *Regime: High‐Energy Surf Zone Currents and Headland Rips at a Geologically Constrained Mesotidal Beach.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Arthur Mouragues 2020, [doi:10.1029/2020jc016259](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020jc016259))
- **C1216.** Four North Sea field datasets indicate transitions between undertow and rip-cell circulation depend jointly on onshore wave mass transport, rip spacing and trough cross-section, separating regimes in a simple parameter space. *Regime: Cross-Shore Currents in the Surf Zone: Rips or Undertow?.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Troels Aagaard 2008, [doi:10.2112/04-0357.1](https://doi.org/10.2112/04-0357.1))
- **C1352.** Among 407 UK beach users, 263 showed poor rip-current knowledge, while 314 understood safety flags and 339 complied; prior education improved knowledge and lifeguards were the most effective source. *Regime: Public Understanding and Knowledge of Rip Currents and Beach Safety in the UK.* [direct_finding, field] (Eleanor Woodward 2015, [doi:10.1123/ijare.2014-0067](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijare.2014-0067))
- **C1353.** Video and buoy analysis at Alassio associated rips with Hs 0.5–1.34 m, mean periods 4.7–7.0 s and directions 150–227°, yielding high annual hazard but moderate tourist-season hazard. *Regime: Rip current hazard assessment on a sandy beach in Liguria, NW Mediterranean.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Luigi Mucerino 2020, [doi:10.1007/s11069-020-04299-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11069-020-04299-9))
