# Mangrove hydrodynamics

`ecohydraulics.mangrove` | Flow and sediment through mangroves.

Parent: [Coastal ecosystems and ecohydraulics](ecohydraulics.md)

Papers: 26. Claims: 25. Equations: 0.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Mangroves dissipate waves and alter currents through form drag, turbulence, blockage, sheltering, and bed friction, while creeks can bypass forest resistance and redistribute water levels.

**Governing physics.** Controls include submerged frontal area, stem/root density and diameter, porosity, flexibility, vertical leaf distribution, relative water depth, Reynolds regime, forest width, channels, bathymetry, and sediment feedback.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Key quantities include drag coefficient, vegetation and root Reynolds numbers, relative submergence, frontal-area density, porosity, blockage, forest-width-to-wavelength scale, applied bed stress, and sediment availability.

**Major equations.** Models use vegetation drag and wave-energy sinks, porous-canopy momentum exchange, turbulence closures, frontal-area and bulk-drag relations, surge-wave coupling, and bio-morphodynamic sediment conservation.

**Typical methods.** Evidence spans natural-forest velocity arrays, storm and erosion surveys, prototype and reduced flumes, detailed geometry scanning, field-calibrated drag, coupled surge-wave models, and long-term bio-morphodynamics.

**Numerical models.** CH3D-SWAN, SWAN-type vegetation dissipation, 3-D vegetation-resolving surge models, porous-root flow models, delta-scale hydrodynamics, and bio-morphodynamic models span event to sea-level-rise scales.

**Experimental datasets.** Accessible cases include Pearl River typhoons, Hurricane Andrew/Ike/Harvey, Typhoon Mangkhut, New Zealand creek systems, Australian temperate mangroves, Mumbai waves, natural pneumatophore turbulence, and Rhizophora flumes.

**Validated ranges.** Case results include about 20% transmission through a 75 m Rhizophora forest, 77% reduction over 100 m during Mangkhut, nearly 70% over 100 m temperate forest, high-water gradients of 12-36 cm/km, and drag formulations with explicit Reynolds bounds.

**Recent advances.** Recent work quantifies prototype drag, geometry-measurement uncertainty, nonlinear low-cover erosion benefits, age-dependent failure, field-calibrated Rhizophora coefficients, delta-wide placement effects, and climate-driven morphology.

**Disagreements.** Mangroves do not uniformly attenuate every hazard: short waves are more consistently reduced than long surge, low-drag channels can eliminate forest-scale attenuation, foreshore breaking can exceed forest contribution, and poorly located patches can amplify water elsewhere.

**Limitations.** Drag coefficients and frontal areas depend on measurement method, species, age, water depth, damage, flexibility, scale, and event. Many studies remain model- or site-specific, and vertical structure and channel networks are often simplified.

**Open questions.** Needs include extreme-event drag and failure, flexible and broken vegetation, channel-forest exchange, wave-surge-tide interaction, evolving bathymetry, sediment budgets, species transfer, uncertainty, and coupled ecological persistence.

**Seminal papers.** The branch develops from mangrove wave-propagation and vegetation-drag theory toward parameterized-tree experiments, vegetation-resolving storm models, field turbulence, drag scaling, and bio-morphodynamic design.
