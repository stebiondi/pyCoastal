## Synthesis

**Well established.** Nonlinear coastal waves exchange energy and phase among harmonics and spectral components; shoaling, breaking, finite steepness, bathymetry, vegetation, currents, and wavemaker boundary conditions alter wave shape and spectra beyond linear superposition.

**Governing physics.** Key mechanisms are quadratic triad and four-wave coupling, free-bound harmonic interference, amplitude and phase modulation, shoaling-driven skewness and asymmetry, breaking-induced decorrelation, nonlinear dispersion, vegetation drag, and nonlinear force generation.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Important controls include relative depth kh or kp h, Ursell number, wave steepness, relative amplitude H/h, spectral bandwidth, bicoherence, biphase, normalized harmonic energy, vegetation density and relative submergence, and Keulegan-Carpenter number for loads.

**Major equations.** The evidence uses bispectra, bicoherence and biphase, Boussinesq spectral evolution with sum and difference triads, eigenfunction-FFT wavemaker solutions, four-phase harmonic separation, WRT four-wave interaction integrals, Ursell scaling, and Stokes-like harmonic load reconstruction.

**Typical methods.** Methods combine controlled regular, random, and focused flume waves; field event records; wavelet and Fourier spectra; bicoherence and bispectra; harmonic phase separation; amplitude-scaled recomputation; semi-analytical wavemaker theory; Boussinesq and fully nonlinear potential-flow models.

**Numerical models.** Models include a semi-analytical eigenfunction-FFT nonlinear wavemaker, OceanWave3D, a spectral Madsen-Sorensen Boussinesq model, WRT four-wave integration, vegetation attenuation formulations, and fully nonlinear potential-flow plus reduced-order harmonic load models.

**Experimental datasets.** Reviewed evidence includes JONSWAP waves over a sill at 0.38<=kp h<=1.22, DeRisk nonlinear irregular waves at two depths, a 64 m transient-wave flume, Tainan H=0.05-0.40 m and T=2-5 s shoaling tests, a mistral event record, vegetation attenuation tests, and focused cylinder-load experiments.

**Validated ranges.** Direct ranges include kp h=0.38-1.22 over a sill; low, moderate, and steep waves at four periods in a 64 m flume; Tainan H=0.05-0.40 m and T=2-5 s; two long-crested irregular-wave depths; Ur about 50-250 for vegetation sensitivity; and nonbreaking inertia-dominated focused cylinder waves.

**Recent advances.** Recent work separates higher-order free and bound content through multi-phase and amplitude-scaled analysis, reconstructs irregular spectra and structural loads, and interprets shoaling beats through joint frequency-wavenumber free-bound decompositions.

**Disagreements.** Spectral peaks can reflect physical nonlinear transfer or free harmonics generated by an inadequate wavemaker prescription. Frequency-domain energy exchange may appear paradoxical when local linear wavelength concepts are applied to spatially nonperiodic free-bound interference.

**Limitations.** Limitations include unidirectional flumes, finite records, spectral leakage, estimator confidence, breaking nonstationarity, wavemaker artifacts, sparse spatial gauges, model truncation order, inviscid assumptions, and regime-specific separation between free and bound components.

**Open questions.** Priorities include multidirectional and current-affected phase coupling, robust separation near breaking, uncertainty-aware bicoherence, nonlinear generation without spurious content, transfer from flumes to storms, and efficient operational approximations to first-principles four-wave interactions.

**Seminal papers.** Within this branch, the 2006 WRT study provides a first-principles four-wave reference, while the 2008 transient wavemaker and sill-bicoherence studies established controlled generation and time-local quadratic-coupling diagnostics.

## Equations

### Biphase

$$
\phi=\tan^{-1}[\operatorname{Im}B(\omega_1,\omega_2)/\operatorname{Re}B(\omega_1,\omega_2)]
$$

Regime: Tainan experiment with H=0.05-0.40 m, T=2-5 s, 21 gauges, 50 Hz sampling, and a 160 m domain, plus the cited field and numerical cases.

Variables: `B` bispectrum; `omega` angular frequency

Source: (Kuznetsov 2021, [doi:10.3390/fluids6100347](https://doi.org/10.3390/fluids6100347))

## Claims

- **C231.** For random waves over a sill with 0.38<=kp h<=1.22, quadratic phase coupling increased during shoaling; it peaked at the sill crest for nonbreaking waves but immediately before incipient breaking for breaking waves, then decreased sharply. *Regime: Two random-wave cases over a sill with 0.38<=kp h<=1.22..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Dong 2008, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2008.02.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2008.02.015))
- **C232.** In two highly nonlinear long-crested irregular-wave experiments, paddle-generated second-order free waves produced spatially nonuniform spectral fluctuations strongest at the smaller depth; force spectra changed similarly, but peak-force statistics were less affected. *Regime: Two long-crested irregular-wave experiments at two depths; strongest distortion at the smaller depth..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Pierella 2021, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2020.103816](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2020.103816))
- **C233.** A semi-analytical nonlinear wavemaker model reasonably reproduced measured elevation and Fourier amplitudes, while linear or weakly nonlinear theory was adequate only for low-steepness waves and could generate disruptive multi-peak spectra at higher steepness. *Regime: Low, moderate, and steep wave trains across four basic periods in a 64 m by 0.6 m flume..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Sulisz 2008, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2007.07.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2007.07.002))
- **C234.** Across field, laboratory, and Boussinesq analyses, spatial beats in shoaling waves were explained by interference among free and bound first and second harmonics; the Tainan tests covered H=0.05-0.40 m and T=2-5 s. *Regime: Tainan experiment with H=0.05-0.40 m, T=2-5 s, 21 gauges, 50 Hz sampling, and a 160 m domain, plus the cited field and numerical cases..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Kuznetsov 2021, [doi:10.3390/fluids6100347](https://doi.org/10.3390/fluids6100347))
- **C235.** For a measured mistral wind-wave record, five-minute wavelet-bicoherence segments revealed temporal details of sum- and difference-frequency coupling that were lost when the full record was averaged. *Regime: One measured record containing the event maximum wave, evaluated as the full record and five-minute segments..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Elsayed 2008, [doi:10.2112/05-0445.1](https://doi.org/10.2112/05-0445.1))
- **C1723.** A Haar-wavelet multiresolution formulation evaluates the WRT nonlinear quadruplet transfer integral with constant weights and equally spaced nodes; one- and two-dimensional tests converge toward benchmark nonlinear wave-wave interaction results as resolution increases. *Regime: Deep-water discrete wind-wave spectra with resonant four-wave interactions represented through WRT geometry..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Uma 2016, [doi:10.1007/s40722-015-0041-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40722-015-0041-3))
