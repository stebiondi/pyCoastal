# Flood-map uncertainty

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Flood maps are conditional estimates, not exact boundaries: elevation, forcing, roughness, defenses, model physics, grid scale, wetting/drying, observations, classification, and climate assumptions all contribute uncertainty.

**Governing physics.** Coastal uncertainty is strongly shaped by dynamic surge and wave timing, tide phase, compound sources, topographic connectivity and slope, friction and obstacles, defense behavior, and the duration available for flood propagation.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Useful controls include elevation error relative to local relief/freeboard, grid spacing relative to terrain scale, forcing error relative to flood threshold, hydrograph duration relative to filling time, roughness sensitivity, and ensemble spread relative to mapped depth.

**Major equations.** Methods combine shallow-water inundation, static connectivity, return-level statistics, spatial elevation-error models, Monte Carlo ensembles, surrogate/multifidelity UQ, data assimilation, classification thresholds, and validation contingency metrics.

**Typical methods.** Approaches include DTM/DSM/SRTM comparison, plausible DEM realizations, static-dynamic model intercomparison, surge-wave omission tests, event and satellite validation, ensemble/multimodel forecasts, Monte Carlo flood zones, and SAR calibration.

**Numerical models.** Static bathtub and connectivity tools, HAND, local-inertial and full dynamic solvers, GeoClaw, global 30 m models, coupled hydrologic-hydrodynamic systems, Monte Carlo, Kriging, and multifidelity methods provide differing fidelity and cost.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes Xynthia, Venice extremes, global tropical cyclones, tsunami DEM cases, Greater London and Tottenville terrain, Sarasota elevation sources, satellite floodplains for 71 storms, and multi-event Sentinel-1 maps.

**Validated ranges.** Reported effects include 209-232% static overprediction at Xynthia, about 60% underprediction when waves are omitted, more than 20% extent sensitivity to surge timing, 31% exposure underestimation without tropical cyclones, and 99.9% Kriging cost reduction.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances quantify hazard-source omission, separate surge-shape effects on depth and extent, generate spatial DEM ensembles, validate global dynamic inundation against satellites, accelerate probability propagation, and stabilize multi-event SAR thresholds.

**Disagreements.** Greater model complexity can improve process representation yet forcing error may still dominate. DTM can overpredict while DSM underpredicts, static methods can grossly overmap, and rapid approximations may be adequate for regional screening but unsafe for cell-level decisions.

**Limitations.** Validation outlines contain sensor and classification error; exposure is not hazard skill; global and local results are not interchangeable; correlated elevation/forcing errors, defenses, compound events, nonstationarity, and rare-event scarcity remain weakly constrained.

**Open questions.** Needs include correlated spatial error, dynamic defenses and failure, joint tide-surge-wave-rain-river ensembles, wetting/drying and urban drainage, reliable depth/timing validation, observation uncertainty, climate nonstationarity, and decision-specific calibration.

**Seminal papers.** The branch develops from DEM and hydraulic-model uncertainty reviews through probabilistic elevation and forcing ensembles to process-based global validation, multifidelity UQ, and uncertainty-aware satellite mapping.

## Claims

- **C597.** Flood-map uncertainty begins with DEM vertical error, resolution, interpolation, hydrologic conditioning, and uncertainty in terrain parameters derived from elevation. *Regime: Elevation error, grid resolution, interpolation, terrain modification, and derived parameters propagate into distributed flood models..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Suzanne P. Wechsler 2007, [doi:10.5194/hess-11-1481-2007](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-11-1481-2007))
- **C598.** Large-scale coastal comparisons found static methods overestimated Xynthia inundation by 209-232%, while omitting waves underestimated flooded area by about 60%. *Regime: Static methods overestimated Xynthia extent by 209-232%; pan-European static extent was 56% above dynamic LFP, while omitting waves underestimated area about 60% and exposure differed 20-70%..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Michalis Vousdoukas 2016, [doi:10.5194/nhess-16-1841-2016](https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-16-1841-2016))
- **C599.** NWM-HAND evaluation identified roughness, streamflow bias above 60% MAE, and low relief as major boundary-error controls and cautioned against cell-scale wet/dry use. *Regime: Roughness was the most important correction parameter; errors persisted above 60% streamflow MAE and in low relief, so HAND was useful for regional risk but not cell wet/dry decisions..* [direct_finding, mixed] (J. Michael Johnson 2019, [doi:10.5194/nhess-19-2405-2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-19-2405-2019))
- **C600.** Adding tropical cyclones more than doubled estimated population exposed to 1-in-1000-year coastal flooding from 78 million to 192 million and corrected a 31% underestimate. *Regime: Including tropical cyclones raised estimated 1-in-1000-year exposed population from 78 million to 192 million; prior exposure was underestimated 31%..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Job Dullaart 2021, [doi:10.1038/s43247-021-00204-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-021-00204-9))
- **C601.** Flood inundation uncertainty arises from hydrologic forcing, validation, topography, steady-flow assumptions, cross-section geometry, and model dimensionality, with 1-D polygon uncertainty increasing away from channels. *Regime: Steady-flow assumptions matter more at high flow, cross-section geometry adds errors, and one-dimensional polygon uncertainty grows away from the main channel..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Bales 2009, [doi:10.1111/j.1753-318x.2009.01029.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1753-318x.2009.01029.x))
- **C602.** Greater London bathtub maps showed DTM-versus-DSM processing differences can dominate nominal RMSE and that SRTM underestimated flood extent. *Regime: DTM-versus-DSM inundation differences exceeded their RMSE differences, and SRTM underestimated extent enough to be unreliable for risk mapping..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Ali P. Yunus 2016, [doi:10.3390/rs8050366](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs8050366))
- **C603.** Venice flood forecasting uncertainty calls for improved meteorological forcing, ensemble prediction, water-level data assimilation, and multimodel approaches. *Regime: Priority improvements are meteorological forecasts, ensembles, water-level assimilation, and multimodel prediction, informed by difficult historic extremes..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Georg Umgiesser 2021, [doi:10.5194/nhess-21-2679-2021](https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-21-2679-2021))
- **C604.** Tsunami case studies found a 25 m mesh adequate with accurate elevation, while DTM tended to overestimate and DSM underestimate inundation under constant roughness. *Regime: A 25 m mesh sufficed with accurate elevation; DTM tended to overestimate and DSM underestimate when constant Manning roughness was used..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Jonathan Griffin 2015, [doi:10.3389/feart.2015.00032](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2015.00032))
- **C605.** A 30 m global model found low-emission flood-hazard change remained within present uncertainty, whereas high-emission change emerged in the 2040s and reached 49% with a 7-109% likely range by 2100. *Regime: SSP1-2.6 end-century change of 9% (-6% to 29%) remained within current hazard uncertainty, while SSP5-8.5 reached 49% (7% to 109%) and emerged from noise in the 2040s..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Oliver Wing 2024, [doi:10.1029/2023wr036460](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023wr036460))
- **C606.** Hydrologic-hydrodynamic comparisons found flood-map accuracy remained dominated by forcing magnitude and timing even as model complexity increased. *Regime: Greater physical complexity did not remove dominant forcing magnitude/timing errors, and delta applications also require tides and surge..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Jannis Hoch 2019, [doi:10.5194/nhess-19-1723-2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-19-1723-2019))
- **C607.** Changing storm-surge temporal variability altered mean and maximum depth by about 5% but flood extent by more than 20% in the studied coastal setting. *Regime: Mean and maximum depths differed about 5%, but extent deviations exceeded 20%, showing timing affects map footprint more than some depth/exposure metrics..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Jorid Höffken 2020, [doi:10.3389/fmars.2020.00098](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2020.00098))
- **C608.** A Sarasota coastal DEM study generated spatial uncertainty surfaces and plausible elevation ensembles for propagating sparse offshore and dense LiDAR uncertainty into coastal models. *Regime: Uncertainty was high offshore where soundings were sparse and low on LiDAR-covered flat land; plausible DEM ensembles propagate elevation uncertainty into process models..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Christopher Amante 2018, [doi:10.2112/jcoastres-d-17-00211.1](https://doi.org/10.2112/jcoastres-d-17-00211.1))
- **C609.** Monte Carlo mapping in low-lying Tottenville showed small elevation and water-level errors can produce large uncertainty in future 1% flood-zone extent. *Regime: Small land/water-height changes produced large extent uncertainty on flat terrain; probabilistic maps represented ranges rather than a single deterministic boundary..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Christopher Amante 2019, [doi:10.1080/13658816.2019.1635253](https://doi.org/10.1080/13658816.2019.1635253))
- **C610.** Across 71 tropical cyclones, global flood models had only moderate satellite-validated skill, while process-based surge-inundation dynamics improved mapped extent. *Regime: All global approaches had moderate skill, but process-based GeoClaw outperformed methods without dynamic inundation..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Thomas Vogt 2024, [doi:10.1038/s43247-024-01707-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-024-01707-x))
- **C611.** A flood-inundation UQ comparison reported Kriging reduced computational cost by 99.9% relative to full Monte Carlo. *Regime: Kriging reduced computational cost by 99.9% relative to full Monte Carlo, illustrating a speed-accuracy route for probabilistic mapping..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Aitken 2024, [doi:10.3390/w16091309](https://doi.org/10.3390/w16091309))
- **C612.** Multi-event Sentinel-1 mapping found threshold selection dominated flood-map uncertainty, whereas stability-optimized calibration limited post-processing variability. *Regime: Threshold choice dominated variability, while stable calibration limited residual post-processing uncertainty and supported coherent cross-event extent/depth patterns..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Khanh 2026, [doi:10.1088/2515-7620/ae88de](https://doi.org/10.1088/2515-7620/ae88de))
- **C1171.** For the 2016 Brazos flood, HAND and iRIC-FaSTMECH extents overlapped Landsat observations by 56% and 70%; adding inter-catchment behavior raised modified HAND overlap to 67%. *Regime: Comparative Analysis of Inundation Mapping Approaches for the 2016 Flood in the Brazos River, Texas.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Jiaqi Zhang 2018, [doi:10.1111/1752-1688.12623](https://doi.org/10.1111/1752-1688.12623))
- **C1172.** For southwest Florida, climate-model, downscaling and scenario choices yield 2100 1%-annual-chance inundation metrics about 3–7 times current values, while today's 100-year event could become a 3-year event. *Regime: A sensitivity study of rising compound coastal inundation over large flood plains in a changing climate.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Y. Peter Sheng 2022, [doi:10.1038/s41598-022-07010-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-07010-z))
- **C1682.** Propagating spatially variable and correlated LiDAR DEM error through 1,000 sequential-Gaussian simulations produced a coastal inundation area about 11% larger than deterministic bathtub mapping at the 1% exceedance level. *Regime: Coastal LiDAR DEM and ground-control data for the Tully region of northeastern Australia under the specified 1 m sea-level-rise plus 100-year storm scenario..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Leon 2014, [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0108727](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0108727))
- **C1738.** For the studied large rural flood, LISFLOOD-FP gained little deterministic skill below 50 m grid spacing, degraded above 50 m, and produced less reliable probabilistic inundation maps beyond 100 m; filtering to only behavioural simulations could also create overconfident maps. *Regime: A large rural flood in the Imera basin, Sicily, represented at 10-500 m grid spacing with uncertain inflow, Manning friction, DEM aggregation, and water-depth observations..* [direct_finding, numerical] (James Savage 2015, [doi:10.1002/hyp.10749](https://doi.org/10.1002/hyp.10749))
- **C1740.** LISFLOOD-FP sensitivity is output-, place-, and time-dependent: inflow dominates flood extent on the rising limb, channel friction near peak inundation, and floodplain friction during recession, while grid resolution and DEM resampling are especially influential for local water depths. *Regime: Rural flood-inundation modelling with uncertain inflow, channel and floodplain Manning friction, spatial resolution, and LiDAR-derived DEM aggregation..* [direct_finding, numerical] (James Savage 2016, [doi:10.1002/2015wr018198](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015wr018198))
