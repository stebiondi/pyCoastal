# Wind-wave growth

`waves.generation.wind` | Growth under wind forcing.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Wind waves grow through wave-coherent pressure work and form drag, with spectral input redistributed by nonlinear interactions and balanced by whitecapping and other dissipation; growth depends on fetch, duration, wave age, direction, and atmospheric variability.

**Governing physics.** Phillips-type turbulent pressure initiates waves; Miles critical layers and separated airflow contribute coherent pressure work; capillary roughness controls low-wind stress; nonlinear four-wave transfer moves energy; breaking limits young-sea growth.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include wave age c/u*, inverse wave age U10/cp, nondimensional fetch and duration, nondimensional energy and peak frequency, directional wind–wave angle, wave steepness, Reynolds number, drag coefficient, gustiness, and breaking strength.

**Major equations.** Core frameworks are the spectral action-balance/Hasselmann equation with wind-input, nonlinear-transfer, whitecapping and swell-loss terms; Miles stability growth; Phillips stochastic forcing; pressure–slope work; wave-boundary-layer stress; and self-similar fetch/duration laws.

**Typical methods.** Methods combine wave-following pressure and airflow, directional wave arrays, SWIFT and acoustic dissipation, video breaking detection, laser imaging and PIV, wind-wave tanks, DNS/LES, phase-resolving simulations, source-term budget closure, and WAVEWATCH III/SWAN hindcasts.

**Numerical models.** Models include WAVEWATCH III ST4/ST6 and ZRP terms, SWAN with a wave-boundary-layer model, Hasselmann-equation solvers, high-resolution DNS/LES, three-dimensional phase-resolving potential flow, coastal atmosphere–wave coupling, and unified directional spectra.

**Experimental datasets.** Key evidence spans Lake George, North Carolina 4–83 km fetch, Ekofisk, Gulf of Tehuantepec, R/P FLIP HiRes, Gorky Reservoir, Kanmon Strait, typhoon observations, repeated 30-state JONSWAP tank tests, and global buoy/satellite validation.

**Validated ranges.** Evidence includes strongly forced U10/cp above earlier sub-3 measurements, North Carolina winds 9–11 m/s over 4–83 km fetch, low-wind capillary regimes below 5–7 m/s, a roughly 50% breaking record, 30 repeated sea states, and global hindcasts through 1979–2019.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances directly observe wave-coherent pressure work and airflow separation, resolve the initial Phillips stage by DNS, diagnose Miles growth experimentally, quantify gustiness, recalibrate global ST6 hindcasts, and couple source terms to regional storm atmospheres.

**Disagreements.** Competing input theories emphasize turbulent pressure, critical layers, or separation; linear critical-layer evidence is strong in controlled regimes but energetic ocean observations also show separation; source-term packages can match bulk height while disagreeing at high frequencies or stress partition.

**Limitations.** Direct pressure and airflow measurements are rare, especially in hurricanes; capillary, spray, stability, current, gust and directional effects are incompletely coupled; dissipation is difficult to measure; and calibration compensation can yield correct bulk waves for incorrect reasons.

**Open questions.** Priorities include regime transitions among Phillips, Miles and separation mechanisms; gust and stability effects; wave-current growth; directional spreading; high-wind drag saturation; spectral breaking closure; capillary-to-gravity coupling; and transferable uncertainty-aware source terms.

**Seminal papers.** The branch links Phillips initial forcing and Miles critical-layer theory to form-drag measurements, Hasselmann spectral balance, self-similar weak turbulence, source-term packages, wave-boundary-layer coupling, and direct pressure-work observations.
