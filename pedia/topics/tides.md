# Tides and coastal circulation

`tides` | Astronomical tides and coastal-scale circulation.

Subtopics: [Tidal propagation](tides.astronomical.md), [Residual circulation](tides.residual.md), [Stratified coastal circulation](tides.stratified.md)

Papers: 10. Claims: 2. Equations: 0.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Astronomical forcing produces predictable barotropic constituents, but coastal amplitude, phase, currents, asymmetry and residual transport depend strongly on depth, friction, geometry and nonlinear interactions. Stratification converts barotropic energy into internal tides that can radiate and mix remotely.

**Governing physics.** The tide-generating potential forces shallow-water motion; friction, convergence, resonance and Coriolis alter propagation. Constituent interaction generates harmonics and residuals, while sloping topography and stratification generate internal waves whose refraction and dissipation depend on background flow.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include tidal amplitude-to-depth ratio, friction and convergence parameters, basin length relative to tidal wavelength, Rossby and Froude numbers, spring–neap ratio, constituent amplitude and phase ratios, skewness, internal-wave slope criticality and conversion/dissipation efficiency.

**Major equations.** Barotropic tides follow depth-integrated mass and momentum equations with astronomical boundary forcing, bottom stress and nonlinear advection. Harmonic analysis decomposes constituents; internal tides use stratified wave and energy equations, and residual circulation emerges through period averaging and nonlinear terms.

**Typical methods.** Studies use tide gauges, current meters, shipboard hydrography and moorings with harmonic, skewness, flux and energy-budget analysis; shallow-water, 3D circulation and nonhydrostatic/internal-wave models test geometry, stratification, friction and coupled mean-level/surge/wave effects.

**Numerical models.** Representations range from constituent shallow-water solvers and COHERENS-like circulation systems to global nonuniform ocean–tide models and internal-wave theory. Coastal-flood models may couple mean level, tides, surge, waves and inundation rather than linearly superpose them.

**Experimental datasets.** Reviewed evidence includes Monterey Canyon ship surveys and monthlong records, Sea of Okhotsk constituent simulations, California mixed-tide estuaries, Paopao reef-lagoon observations, Changjiang circulation literature, and realistic/idealized Northwest Pacific tide experiments.

**Validated ranges.** Examples include Monterey internal currents near 20 cm/s and 50 m displacement, Okhotsk residuals near 10 cm/s, Mid-Atlantic Ridge M2 flux 3–5 mW/m² and coastal interaction effects of tens of centimeters. These belong to distinct regimes and scales.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances couple global circulation, eddies and internal tides at variable resolution, diagnose nonlinear energy budgets, use skewness for mixed-tide asymmetry and embed tide–surge–wave–mean-level interactions in probabilistic coastal flooding.

**Disagreements.** Linear constituent superposition is useful but can miss nonlinear water-level, friction, flooding and harmonic interactions. Internal-tide energy may dissipate near generation sites or radiate far away depending on mode and topography; idealized stratification can bias local conversion materially.

**Limitations.** Sparse offshore observations, uncertain bathymetry and drag, datum and nodal corrections, unresolved channels, changing morphology and mean level, incomplete stratification and background currents, and model resolution constrain prediction and attribution.

**Open questions.** Priorities include climate-driven tidal change, compound extremes, wetting/drying feedback, estuary asymmetry and sediment response, internal-tide remote dissipation, eddy interaction, data assimilation and consistent uncertainty across barotropic and baroclinic scales.

**Seminal papers.** Equilibrium and dynamic tide theory established constituents and propagation; harmonic analysis enabled prediction, while shallow-water theory explained amplification and friction. Later work formalized tidal asymmetry, residual circulation and barotropic-to-baroclinic conversion.

## Claims

- **C1245.** Coastal tidal prediction must account for interactions with mean sea level, surge, waves and inundation whenever their locally variable contributions are comparable to the water-level margin relevant to flooding or design. *Regime: Coastal water levels and flooding where coupled processes may alter elevation by leading-order amounts..* [literature_review_statement, review] (Déborah Idier 2019, [doi:10.1007/s10712-019-09549-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10712-019-09549-5))
- **C1587.** A quasi-global tide-gauge analysis separates total-water-level and surge maxima to assess spatial and temporal change in coastal extreme high waters. *Regime: Changes in extreme high water levels based on a quasi‐global tide‐gauge data set.* [direct_finding, field] (Melisa Menéndez 2010, [doi:10.1029/2009jc005997](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009jc005997))
