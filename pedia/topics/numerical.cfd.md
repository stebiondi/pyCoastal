## Synthesis

**Well established.** Credible coastal CFD requires separate verification of conservation, discretization, interface transport and wave boundaries plus validation against measurements for the target processes; agreement in surface elevation alone does not establish turbulence, pressure, load or transport accuracy.

**Governing physics.** Coastal CFD resolves incompressible momentum and continuity with gravity, viscosity and turbulence while tracking air–water and sometimes sediment or granular phases; nonlinear waves, breaking, runup, overtopping, entrained air, currents and moving or porous boundaries create strong multiscale coupling.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Key controls include Reynolds, Froude, Weber and Keulegan–Carpenter numbers, relative depth and steepness, Courant number, cells per wavelength and wave height, y-plus, interface thickness, reflection coefficient, mass error, grid-convergence index and runtime or cell-count ratios.

**Major equations.** Core equations are Navier–Stokes mass and momentum conservation, Reynolds-averaged or filtered turbulence equations, phase-fraction advection for VOF/CLSVOF, constitutive stress or rheology for additional phases, and kinematic/dynamic free-surface and wave-generation boundary conditions.

**Typical methods.** A defensible workflow verifies still water and conservation, calibrates wave generation and absorption, performs mesh/time-step and domain tests, compares turbulence and interface schemes, validates kinematics and integral outputs against laboratory data, audits reflection and mass balance, then reports cost and uncertainty.

**Numerical models.** The evidence spans OpenFOAM interFoam, multiPhaseInterFoam and sedInterFoam, ANSYS Fluent, DualSPHysics, SWASH, HOS-NWT, fully nonlinear potential solvers, RANS k-epsilon and k-omega, LES, VOF and CLSVOF, plus one- and two-way domain decomposition.

**Experimental datasets.** Reviewed benchmarks include dike overtopping, regular deep-water propagation, steep focused groups, dam break, solitary runup, submerged obstacles, shallow-foreshore transformation, numerical-flume turbulence tests, irregular-wave generation and multiphase sediment or landslide cases.

**Validated ranges.** Validation covers selected two- and three-dimensional flume-scale regular, solitary, focused, irregular, overtopping, breaking and multiphase cases; solver performance remains conditional on grid, boundary treatment, turbulence closure, phase model and application geometry.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances use coupled potential–viscous domains, cross-solver benchmark campaigns, multiphase rheology, three-phase air–water–sediment models, improved interface schemes, realistic spectral boundaries and explicit accuracy–cost intercomparison across open-source solvers.

**Disagreements.** No solver is uniformly superior: viscous multiphase CFD resolves breaking and local loads but is costly; potential, spectral and nonhydrostatic models are efficient away from strongly viscous regions; RANS, LES, VOF, CLSVOF and SPH trade dissipation, interface sharpness, robustness and resolution differently.

**Limitations.** Persistent limitations include numerical diffusion and reflection, mass imbalance, grid and time-step sensitivity, turbulence overproduction beneath waves, uncertain air entrainment and compressibility, expensive three-dimensional resolution, closure dependence, boundary inconsistency and sparse validation of local extremes.

**Open questions.** Priorities include conservative sharp interfaces, robust breaking turbulence, air-pocket compressibility, adaptive meshes, porous and moving media, flexible coupling, sediment feedback, uncertainty quantification, reproducible high-performance implementation and field-scale validation.

**Seminal papers.** Finite-volume Navier–Stokes solvers, Reynolds averaging, LES, VOF free-surface capture and numerical wave tanks established modern coastal CFD; absorbing boundaries, wave-generation toolboxes and formal verification/validation made engineering applications increasingly reproducible.

- **C1105.** A multiPhaseInterFoam solver represents granular landslide tsunamis with Coulomb viscoplastic rheology and a small set of physical parameters, validated against generation and propagation evidence. *Regime: Numerical modeling of tsunamis generated by granular landslides in OpenFOAM®: A Coulomb viscoplastic rheology.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Alessandro Romano 2023, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2023.104391](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2023.104391))

- **C1111.** sedInterFoam adds an air phase and VOF free surface to a water–sediment Eulerian solver and validates interface evolution, phase mass conservation and predictive behavior across four tests. *Regime: sedInterFoam 1.0: a three-phase numerical model for sediment transport applications with free surfaces.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Antoine Mathieu 2025, [doi:10.5194/gmd-18-1561-2025](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-18-1561-2025))
