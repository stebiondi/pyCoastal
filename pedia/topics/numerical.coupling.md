# Model coupling and nesting

`numerical.coupling` | Multi-model and multi-scale coupling.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Coupling is necessary when feedback or scale interaction affects the answer; a coupled system must be evaluated against uncoupled baselines and observations because individually valid components can exchange inconsistent states, fluxes or boundary information.

**Governing physics.** Coupled coastal systems exchange momentum, mass, energy and tracers among atmosphere, waves, circulation, rivers, inundation and morphology. Feedback can alter wind stress, wave growth, currents, water levels and dissipation rather than merely pass forcing downstream.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include parent-to-child grid ratio, coupling interval relative to process time scale, Courant number, overlap width, relaxation strength, flux or mass imbalance, interpolation error, scale-separation ratio, feedback gain, forecast skill change and parallel efficiency.

**Major equations.** Coupling links component conservation equations through boundary conditions and source terms: stress and heat flux, radiation stress or vortex force, wave action, water level and velocity, discharge, sediment flux and moving geometry, with interpolation, relaxation and conservative remapping operators.

**Typical methods.** Workflows define components and exchanged fields, choose online/offline and one-/two-way direction, harmonize coordinates and conventions, remap states or fluxes, synchronize clocks and restarts, test idealized interfaces and conservation, compare forced and coupled runs, then validate integrated outputs.

**Numerical models.** Reviewed systems include OASIS3-MCT/SURFEX infrastructure, COSMO–WAM, wave-coupled shelf models, FVCOM inundation nesting, TELEMAC wave–3D hydrodynamics, WAVEWATCH III domain decomposition and high-resolution coupled atmosphere–ocean models.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes nested Mediterranean and Adriatic circulation, Hurricane Ike waves, California upwelling, Gulf Stream and Agulhas currents, Scituate nor’easter inundation, southern North Sea forecasts, northwest-European shelf operations and Taranto multi-scale observations.

**Validated ranges.** The reviewed systems span submesoscale coastal currents to global-parent/regional-child hierarchies and event to operational scales; demonstrated skill is conditional on component versions, exchanged fields, grid hierarchy, coupling interval, forcing and regional observations.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances use community middleware, unstructured nested inundation, multi-way wave–ocean–atmosphere exchange, high-resolution current feedback, domain-decomposed parallel waves and validated open-source multiple-nesting workflows for operational coasts.

**Disagreements.** One-way offline nesting is simple and reproducible but omits feedback; two-way online coupling is more physically complete but can amplify interface and component biases. Shared grids ease exchange yet restrict component discretization, while separate grids add remapping and conservation error.

**Limitations.** Major limitations are inconsistent grids and vertical coordinates, nonconservative interpolation, boundary reflection, asynchronous clocks, spin-up shocks, feedback double counting, unresolved scale overlap, component bias compensation, high communication cost, fragile restart provenance and sparse coupled observations.

**Open questions.** Priorities include conservative adaptive remapping, wet/dry nested boundaries, coupled data assimilation, ensemble uncertainty, moving morphology, wave–atmosphere–ocean–river feedback, error attribution, coupling-frequency optimization, standardized metadata and operational fault tolerance.

**Seminal papers.** Nested ocean models established basin-to-coast downscaling, while wave–current and atmosphere–ocean systems introduced online feedback; community couplers and Earth-system interfaces later standardized field exchange, synchronization and parallel execution.

## Claims

- **C136.** The unified wave-generation interface transferred surface-elevation and depth-averaged-velocity time series from MIKE 21 BW to 2D or 3D piston wavemakers while accounting for dispersion, shallow-water nonlinearity, and evanescent modes. *Regime: One-way coupling to DHI-style piston wavemakers; reflected physical waves are not fed back numerically..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Zhang 2007, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2006.08.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2006.08.009))
- **C362.** Across analytical, laboratory, and field benchmarks, the shared-grid SELFE-WWM-II coupling captured wave-current interaction with stable convergence over multiple scales, while the authors identified three-dimensional wave-induced effects as still requiring further research. *Regime: Unstructured-grid multiscale wave-current benchmarks and applications described in the indexed abstract..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Aron Roland 2012, [doi:10.1029/2012jc007952](https://doi.org/10.1029/2012jc007952))
- **C1115.** Coupled California-upwelling simulations show that passing ocean surface currents back to atmospheric stress reduces surface eddy kinetic energy by about half and exposes feedback absent from uncoupled forcing. *Regime: Modulation of Wind Work by Oceanic Current Interaction with the Atmosphere.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Lionel Renault 2016, [doi:10.1175/jpo-d-15-0232.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/jpo-d-15-0232.1))
- **C1116.** A three-level Mediterranean–Adriatic–northern-Adriatic hierarchy uses one-way offline nesting to downscale basin circulation to resolved coastal fields and tests reproduction of known regional features. *Regime: The Adriatic Sea modelling system: a nested approach.* [direct_finding, numerical] (M. Zavatarelli 2003, [doi:10.5194/angeo-21-345-2003](https://doi.org/10.5194/angeo-21-345-2003))
- **C1117.** WAVEWATCH III domain decomposition and an implicit scheme enable high-resolution unstructured hurricane-wave calculations compatible with community coupling infrastructure and validated for Hurricane Ike. *Regime: Large-scale hurricane modeling using domain decomposition parallelization and implicit scheme implemented in WAVEWATCH III wave model.* [direct_finding, numerical] (A. Abdolali 2020, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2020.103656](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2020.103656))
- **C1118.** A SURFEX–OASIS3-MCT interface provides a shared atmosphere-to-ocean, wave, ice and hydrology coupling structure from process and coastal domains to regional and global systems. *Regime: SURFEX v8.0 interface with OASIS3-MCT to couple atmosphere with hydrology, ocean, waves and sea-ice models, from coastal to global scales.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Aurore Voldoire 2017, [doi:10.5194/gmd-10-4207-2017](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-10-4207-2017))
- **C1119.** Coupled Gulf Stream and Agulhas simulations show current feedback produces eddy killing and materially changes boundary-current energy transfers. *Regime: Remarkable Control of Western Boundary Currents by Eddy Killing, a Mechanical Air‐Sea Coupling Process.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Lionel Renault 2019, [doi:10.1029/2018gl081211](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018gl081211))
- **C1120.** High-resolution coupled California simulations show surface-stress feedback damps submesoscale currents, demonstrating that coupling effects persist below mesoscale resolution. *Regime: Dampening of Submesoscale Currents by Air-Sea Stress Coupling in the Californian Upwelling System.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Lionel Renault 2018, [doi:10.1038/s41598-018-31602-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-31602-3))
- **C1121.** A nested FVCOM inundation system for Scituate represents flooding/drying, coastal structures and wave–current effects and reproduces the compound tide–surge flooding of a 2010 nor’easter. *Regime: Coastal flooding in Scituate (MA): A FVCOM study of the 27 December 2010 nor'easter.* [direct_finding, field] (Robert C. Beardsley 2013, [doi:10.1002/2013jc008862](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013jc008862))
- **C1122.** Two-way COSMO–WAM coupling with wave-induced atmospheric drag reduces surface-wind and wave-height forecast errors in the dynamically complex southern North Sea and a nested German Bight grid. *Regime: An atmosphere–wave regional coupled model: improving predictions of wave heights and surface winds in the southern North Sea.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Kathrin Wahle 2017, [doi:10.5194/os-13-289-2017](https://doi.org/10.5194/os-13-289-2017))
- **C1123.** Operational northwest-European shelf experiments demonstrate how wave coupling changes atmosphere–ocean momentum exchange and can improve regional ocean forecasts relative to externally forced mode. *Regime: Can wave coupling improve operational regional ocean forecasts for the north-west European Shelf?.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Huw Lewis 2019, [doi:10.5194/os-15-669-2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/os-15-669-2019))
- **C1124.** A validated multiple-nesting workflow downscales large-scale ocean fields into high-resolution coupled TELEMAC wave–3D hydrodynamics for the Gulf of Taranto and Mar Grande. *Regime: A coupled wave–3-D hydrodynamics model of the Taranto Sea (Italy): amultiple-nesting approach.* [direct_finding, field] (Maria Gabriella Gaeta 2016, [doi:10.5194/nhess-16-2071-2016](https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-16-2071-2016))
- **C1299.** At 18 British tidal-energy sites, 49–93% of waves were oblique to tidal flow; coupled COAWST experiments reduced theoretical tidal resource by about 10% per metre of wave-height increase, conditional on period and direction. *Regime: Realistic wave conditions and their influence on quantifying the tidal stream energy resource.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Matthew Lewis 2014, [doi:10.1016/j.apenergy.2014.09.061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2014.09.061))
- **C1305.** A March 2019 MIKE21 FM wave–hydrodynamic simulation agreed acceptably with Iraqi coastal observations; omitting wind lowered significant wave height by 46.8%, while wave-period Nash efficiency reached 0.92. *Regime: Numerical Simulation of Hydrodynamic and Spectral Model of Iraqi Coastal Water at the Northern Arabian Gulf.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Majid Al-Rammahi 2025, [doi:10.37934/cfdl.17.9.194211](https://doi.org/10.37934/cfdl.17.9.194211))
- **C1375.** An economical surface-wave model retains depth/current refraction and depth-variable coupling and was demonstrated against Hurricane Katrina buoy data and SWAN calculations before full ocean coupling. *Regime: A Surface Wave Model for Coupling with Numerical Ocean Circulation Models.* [direct_finding, mixed] (George L. Mellor 2008, [doi:10.1175/2008jtecho573.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/2008jtecho573.1))
