# River plumes

`estuaries.plumes` | Buoyant coastal discharge.

Parent: [Estuaries and tidal inlets](estuaries.md)

Papers: 33. Claims: 35. Equations: 0.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** River discharge forms buoyant bulges, fronts and coastal currents whose fate reflects competition among inertia, buoyancy, rotation, tides, wind, waves, mixing and shelf geometry.

**Governing physics.** Freshwater spreads radially near the mouth, adjusts geostrophically over a deformation radius and commonly turns into a coastally trapped current. Supercritical outflow forms leading fronts; tides strain and mix the plume; wind-driven Ekman transport compresses, deepens or exports it.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Key controls include internal Froude, Kelvin/source-width-to-deformation-radius, Rossby and Richardson numbers, discharge and buoyancy flux, tidal excursion/source width, wind stress/buoyancy forcing, plume depth/shelf depth and Ekman transport.

**Major equations.** Models use reduced-gravity shallow-water or hydrostatic primitive equations with salinity buoyancy, Coriolis, turbulence closure and surface/bottom stress. Integral salt, volume and freshwater budgets estimate entrainment, flushing and transport.

**Typical methods.** Approaches combine ship and ferry salinity transects, gliders, moorings, HF radar, microstructure, drifters, satellite color/temperature/turbidity, Lagrangian inversion, idealized theory and nested three-dimensional coastal models.

**Numerical models.** The corpus spans reduced-gravity theory, idealized rotating plume models, FVCOM and other primitive-equation shelf models, Lagrangian discharge inversion, front-resolving Rhine simulations and wave–current models for small nearshore outflows.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes Columbia, Fraser, Changjiang, Rhine, Gironde and Winyah Bay plumes; Great Barrier Reef floods; Oregon and Kochi coastal currents; small Southern California streams; and northern Adriatic flood-plume interactions.

**Validated ranges.** Studies cover small creeks to major rivers, weakly rotating to rotational plumes, tidal and nontidal shelves, surface- and bottom-trapped structures, downwelling and upwelling winds, flood pulses to four-year records and near-field to regional scales.

**Recent advances.** Recent work resolves bottom-generated tidal mixing, bottom trapping, wind-driven supercritical export, small-creek sensitivity, high-resolution frontal instability, satellite–Lagrangian discharge inversion and coupled radar/glider/model attribution.

**Disagreements.** Classical surface-trapped coastal-current theory does not encompass bottom-trapped plumes sustained by tidal mixing, upstream spreading, or wind-driven supercritical export; apparent plume boundaries also differ among salinity, temperature and optical tracers.

**Limitations.** Fronts and mixing are intermittent and undersampled, discharge data can be uncertain, satellite proxies are not unique salinity measures, turbulence closures differ, nested boundaries affect transport and idealized theories simplify source geometry and ambient currents.

**Open questions.** Needs include predictive mixing closure, submesoscale frontal instability, wave effects, multiple interacting plumes, sediment and biogeochemical feedback, flood extremes, data assimilation, and robust transfer among source-width, rotation and tidal regimes.

**Seminal papers.** Foundational theory framed radial spreading versus rotational trapping; long river-mouth records and Columbia/Fraser studies then established tidal pulsing, supercritical fronts, bulges and multi-timescale estuarine forcing.
