# Habitat and restoration

Parent: [Coastal ecosystems and ecohydraulics](ecohydraulics.md)

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Building or protecting habitat does not by itself establish ecological function. Outcomes depend on environmental suitability, hydrodynamics and sediment, connectivity and dispersal, structural complexity, target species and life stage, unresolved stressors, restoration age, maintenance, and the reference and metric used to define success.

**Governing physics.** Waves, wakes, tides, inundation, sediment moisture and grain size, erosion and accretion, elevation, and structural porosity or complexity set the physical template; colonization, recruitment, trophic subsidy, growth, mortality, dispersal, and succession determine whether that template becomes functioning habitat.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Useful nondimensional measures include relative elevation and inundation, porosity and relative structural dimensions, habitat or vegetation fraction, standardized effect size, classification agreement, survival or recruitment ratios, carbon additionality and permanence factors, and quality-adjusted habitat units normalized by cost.

**Major equations.** Common formulations include sediment and habitat-state balances, species-density and occupancy models, dispersal-distance relationships, random-effects standardized mean difference, shoreline-rate comparison, vegetation spectral regressions, carbon accounting, and cost per average annual habitat unit.

**Typical methods.** Robust assessment uses BACI or restored-natural-disturbed comparisons, replicated physical and biological surveys, shoreline and elevation monitoring, sediment and water measurements, abundance plus demographic and diet metrics, remote sensing, long-term maintenance records, meta-analysis, spatial prioritization, and lifecycle cost and service accounting.

**Numerical models.** Models include habitat-suitability and colonization regressions, random-effects meta-analysis and moderator tests, spatial conservation prioritization, vegetation biomass regression, point-cloud and habitat classifiers, blue-carbon accounting, and cost-effectiveness per quality-adjusted habitat outcome.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes a 3.5-year created-wetland colonization series at Mugu Lagoon, four years at three paired hybrid-shoreline sites, six Louisiana oyster reefs revisited after ten years, restored reef and living-shoreline fish observations, 133 Gulf restoration projects, and 42 wetland-fisheries studies with 15 suitable for meta-analysis.

**Validated ranges.** Observed regimes include 3.5 years of wetland colonization; four-year shoreline trends from roughly 1 m/y advance to nearly 2 m/y retreat; ten-year oyster density falling below 60 individuals/m2 from early values above 1000/m2; project extents of 0.04-364 ha; and a 42-study review yielding only 15 quantitatively compatible studies.

**Recent advances.** Recent work shifts from acreage and simple abundance toward life-stage habitat function, decade-scale persistence, remote high-resolution habitat condition, systematic evidence synthesis, spatial recovery prioritization, blue-carbon finance, and quality-adjusted restoration cost.

**Disagreements.** Stable or advancing hybrid shorelines and failed decade-scale oyster persistence are conditional, contrasting outcomes rather than a universal verdict on living shorelines. Similar mean fish density in restored and natural sites also does not prove equal fisheries production, because abundance, ontogenetic use, trophic export, and harvest occur at different scales.

**Limitations.** Most evidence covers few sites, taxa, designs, and years; reference sites can be non-equivalent; direct production and trophic subsidy are rare; restoration area is often substituted for quality; physical protection may outlast biological function or vice versa; and carbon and economic valuation require uncertain boundaries and permanence assumptions.

**Open questions.** Priorities are function-specific design rules, seascape connectivity and trophic-subsidy measurement, demographic and fisheries-production outcomes, multi-decadal persistence, adaptive maintenance thresholds, cross-habitat tradeoffs, standardized quality-adjusted metrics, and coupled ecological-hydrodynamic-economic optimization under climate change.

**Seminal papers.** Within this screened branch, the 2004 Mugu Lagoon study demonstrates that habitat suitability and dispersal jointly limit colonization, while the 2018 boulder synthesis frames structural complexity and stability as explicit ecological-engineering design variables.
