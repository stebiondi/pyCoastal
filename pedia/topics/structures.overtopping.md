# Overtopping

`structures.overtopping` | Discharge and consequences of overtopping.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Wave overtopping occurs when runup exceeds crest elevation. Design must consider mean discharge, number of overtopping waves, individual and maximum volumes, velocities and depths, spatial distribution, drainage, tolerable limits, and exposed receptors.

**Governing physics.** Controls include offshore spectrum, breaking and infragravity transformation, water level, foreshore depth/slope/permeability/morphology, obliquity, roughness, porosity, crest freeboard/width, walls, berms, wave steepness, runup, and structure damage.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Important groups include relative freeboard, Iribarren number, wave steepness, relative depth, crest and berm geometry, roughness and obliquity factors, dimensionless discharge q*, overtopping probability, and normalized individual volume.

**Major equations.** Core frameworks normalize mean discharge by gravity and wave scale, use exponential freeboard decay and roughness/geometry factors, model individual volumes with Weibull or exponential distributions, and estimate maxima from mean rate and overtopping-wave count.

**Typical methods.** Methods use long irregular-wave flume series, repeated stochastic realizations, large-scale and field sensing, CLASH/EurOtop databases, empirical regression, phase-averaged and phase-resolving models, CFD/SPH, neural networks, and probability fitting.

**Numerical models.** Models include SWASH, SWAN with infragravity corrections, phase-resolving solvers, empirical EurOtop-type relations, neural and polynomial regressions, and wave-by-wave Weibull/exponential models.

**Experimental datasets.** Reviewed evidence includes CLASH, 124 SWASH validation cases, six-model benchmarks, 140 rubble-seawall tests, 135 individual-volume tests, 1:50 shingle-foreshore tests, repeated random series, and stepped-revetment campaigns.

**Validated ranges.** Reported findings include order-of-magnitude underprediction without infragravity waves, 40% RMSE reduction for a rubble-seawall formula, stochastic discharge ranges to 75%, factor-four mean reduction from permeable shingle, stepped reduction to 60%, and maximum-volume bias improved to -6%.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances resolve infragravity controls, benchmark speed versus accuracy, model complex retrofits, quantify random-series uncertainty, distinguish mean and maximum behavior, and fit wave-by-wave volumes for risk-based design.

**Disagreements.** Mean and maximum metrics can respond differently: permeable foreshores reduced mean discharge strongly without a significant maximum-volume change. Higher-fidelity models are not automatically more accurate if incident conditions or decisive infragravity physics are wrong.

**Limitations.** Scale effects, finite series, stochastic realization, toe-wave reconstruction, morphology, permeability, damage, obliquity, wind, currents, drainage, prototype sensing, and sparse extremes limit transfer. Confidence bounds must accompany formula use.

**Open questions.** Needs include joint discharge-volume-velocity prediction, dynamic morphology and damage, current/wind effects, nonstationary climate, prototype wave-by-wave validation, receptor-specific tolerances, and uncertainty-aware retrofit optimization.

**Seminal papers.** The branch builds on classical runup and freeboard-decay relations, CLASH/EurOtop databases, roughness and geometry factors, and Weibull individual-volume models; current work extends shallow, complex, permeable, and stochastic regimes.

## Claims

- **C407.** For the existing and proposed Port of Las Nieves breakwaters, modeled access-area overtopping risk was substantially higher than along main-body cross sections, identifying the access sections as the priority for controls and expansion design. *Regime: Existing and proposed Port of Las Nieves breakwaters, Gran Canaria..* [direct_finding, numerical] (JAVIER SANTANA-CEBALLOS 2019, [doi:10.2495/ei-v2-n1-59-71](https://doi.org/10.2495/ei-v2-n1-59-71))
- **C408.** For sea dikes on very shallow foreshores, Van Gent's relation overestimated mean discharge and could not treat emergent toes, while an equivalent-slope concept validated with CLASH and dedicated tests improved prediction. *Regime: Shallow, very shallow, and emergent-toe sea-dike conditions..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Corrado Altomare 2016, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2016.07.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2016.07.002))
- **C409.** Across 124 shallow-foreshore cases, SWASH predicted mean overtopping about as accurately as semi-empirical formulae when toe-wave conditions were reproduced and handled complex geometry, although one intense individual event was underpredicted. *Regime: Impermeable structures, shallow foreshores, and complex storm-wall geometries..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Tomohiro Suzuki 2017, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2017.01.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2017.01.009))
- **C410.** In a six-model shallow-foreshore benchmark, neglecting infragravity waves underestimated overtopping by an order of magnitude, while empirically corrected phase-averaged models could match or outperform costlier phase-resolving models. *Regime: Dikes with shallow mildly sloping foreshores..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Lashley 2020, [doi:10.1016/j.envsoft.2020.104740](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2020.104740))
- **C411.** For tested rubble-mound breakwaters, relative protruding crest-wall height dominated wall effects, while berm width, berm level, and wave steepness controlled berm influence on overtopping; expressions were also checked against oblique-wave data. *Regime: Rubble-mound breakwaters with crest walls, berms, or both, including oblique-wave validation..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Marcel R.A. van Gent 2022, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2022.104151](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2022.104151))
- **C412.** Using 140 new tests and existing EurOtop data, a rubble-mound seawall formula based on relative freeboard and wave steepness reduced RMSE by 40% and improved normalized-discharge bias from -0.38 to approximately zero. *Regime: Small-scale rubble-mound seawalls across relative freeboard and steepness conditions..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Ali Koosheh 2022, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2021.104062](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2021.104062))
- **C413.** For smooth-slope and vertical-wall tests, normalized overtopping-discharge variation reached 10% when one wave series was repeated but 75% when different realizations from the same spectrum were used. *Regime: Smooth slope and vertical wall under multiple realizations from identical spectra..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Hannah Williams 2019, [doi:10.3390/jmse7070217](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse7070217))
- **C414.** In 1:50 tests of a 1:2 wall, a permeable shingle foreshore reduced mean overtopping by up to a factor four relative to impermeable-slope predictions, while maximum individual volumes showed no significant difference. *Regime: A 1:2 wall fronted by impermeable versus permeable shingle slopes..* [direct_finding, experimental] (M. Salauddin 2019, [doi:10.1016/j.oceaneng.2019.106866](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2019.106866))
- **C415.** Stepped revetments reduced breaking-wave overtopping volumes by up to 60% relative to smooth slopes, with reduction declining as Iribarren number increased; a new formulation explicitly reports range, scatter, and scale effects. *Regime: Breaking and nonbreaking waves on stepped revetments across disclosed parameter ranges..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Kerpen 2019, [doi:10.3390/w11051035](https://doi.org/10.3390/w11051035))
- **C416.** For tested vertical seawalls, individual overtopping volumes followed a two-parameter Weibull distribution; relative freeboard and overtopping rate predicted shape under nonimpulsive attack, and a unified formula was proposed across impulsive and nonimpulsive cases. *Regime: Plain vertical seawalls across freeboard, depth, steepness, and impulsive/nonimpulsive conditions..* [direct_finding, experimental] (M. Salauddin 2022, [doi:10.1038/s41598-022-20464-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-20464-5))
- **C417.** Across 135 rubble-mound seawall tests, weighted Weibull and exponential fitting reduced scatter by 23% and 17%, while a q*-and-overtopping-wave-count formula achieved 37% scatter index and -6% bias for maximum individual volume. *Regime: Rubble-mound slopes 1:1.5 and 1:2 across practical crest freeboards..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Ali Koosheh 2022, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2022.104173](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2022.104173))
- **C1609.** Across 450 validated SWASH cases for vertical walls on 1:7-1:10 foreshores, longer relative promenades reduced overtopping and a new EurOtop-compatible storm-wall reduction factor reproduced modeled discharge with r = 0.94. *Regime: Vertical walls with retreated storm walls, steep 1:7-1:10 foreshores, and the Mediterranean-scale parameter ranges tested in the paper..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Damjan Bujak 2025, [doi:10.3390/jmse13071285](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse13071285))
- **C1612.** Wave-flume tests of ten roughened vertical-seawall configurations found that increasing protrusion length and density reduced mean overtopping by up to 100% under impulsive waves, while tested non-impulsive waves showed no significant reduction. *Regime: Small-scale vertical seawall tests on a 1:20 impermeable foreshore, within the tested protrusion geometries and impulsive/non-impulsive waves..* [direct_finding, experimental] (M. Salauddin 2021, [doi:10.3389/fmars.2021.674630](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2021.674630))
- **C1616.** Evolutionary Polynomial Regression across five datasets produced parsimonious unified mean-overtopping formulas for smooth dikes through vertical walls, confirming exponential response and improved prediction when water depth, wave period, foreshore slope and dike slope are combined through equivalent or imaginary slope. *Regime: Smooth sea dikes and vertical walls across the combined datasets; rough or permeable structures only for preliminary conservative use..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Corrado Altomare 2020, [doi:10.3390/jmse8080570](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse8080570))
- **C1725.** Large-scale mobile-bed tests show storm-driven lowering of the sandy foreshore before a near-vertical seawall increases overtopping, with total overtopped volume rising by as much as 104%; cumulative volume is more sensitive to profile evolution than peak discharge rate. *Regime: Low-steepness storm sequences over an evolving 1:15 sandy foreshore fronting a near-vertical 10:1 seawall..* [direct_finding, experimental] (Riccardo Briganti 2022, [doi:10.1016/j.oceaneng.2022.112024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2022.112024))
- **C1742.** For the January 2015 Scituate blizzard case, coupled tide–surge–wave interactions increased predicted seawall overtopping about fivefold; a 0.36 m sea-level rise doubled peak discharge, and maintaining baseline discharge required roughly 0.9 m of crest raising because deeper toe water also increased incident wave height. *Regime: Scituate seawall, Massachusetts, during the January 2015 North American blizzard and scenarios combining 0.36 m sea-level rise with raised crest elevations..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Xie 2019, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2019.02.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2019.02.001))
- **C1743.** The SWEEP-OWWL regional forecast system predicted 80% of observed overtopping events one day ahead, all severe events, with a 2.5% false-alarm rate; profile choice changed predicted discharge by an order of magnitude, demonstrating the operational value of frequently updated coastal morphology. *Regime: Engineered and naturally defended profiles along roughly 1,000 km of southwest England, validated against winter 2018/19 observations and tested with Storm Emma boundary conditions..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Kit Stokes 2020, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2020.103827](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2020.103827))
