# Laboratory instrumentation

## Synthesis

**Well established.** No single instrument resolves all coastal laboratory variables: point probes provide bandwidth, imaging provides spatial structure, acoustic methods work in optically difficult flows, and morphology/transport inference needs repeated geometry plus conservation; synchronization and uncertainty are integral to the measurement.

**Governing physics.** Measurements rely on optical absorption, scattering and fluorescence; particle/tracer advection; acoustic travel time and backscatter; stereo triangulation; pressure and pore-pressure transduction; surface reflection/refraction; sampling bandwidth; spatial averaging; and inversion from signals to physical variables.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include pixels or probe diameter relative to feature scale, sampling frequency relative to wave/impact bandwidth, particle Stokes number, image displacement/interrogation window, signal-to-noise ratio, acoustic wavelength and concentration, optical depth, KC number, sensor spacing/wavelength, and uncertainty/response magnitude.

**Major equations.** Core formulations include camera projection and stereo triangulation, PIV/PTV cross-correlation and particle displacement, acoustic Doppler/range inversion, Beer–Lambert and radiative transfer, Exner sediment continuity, sensor transfer functions, sampling/Nyquist criteria, calibration regression, error propagation and repeatability statistics.

**Typical methods.** Methods include capacitance/resistance or ultrasonic surface probes, cameras and photogrammetry, stereo laser-induced fluorescence, PIV/PTV, acoustic Doppler and multibeam mapping, radar and laser ranging, pressure/load/pore-pressure sensors, optical spectroscopy/fluorescence, repeated calibration, hybrid synchronization and numerical cross-validation.

**Numerical models.** Analysis tools include PIV-style cross-correlation applied to acoustic bed maps, Exner conversion to bedload, stereo reconstruction, image processing, optical inversion, uncertainty and repeatability analysis, and CFD used as a spatially complete comparator for PIV measurements.

**Experimental datasets.** Accessible evidence includes laboratory validation of acoustic mapping velocimetry, repeatable plunging-wave impacts with global video and local stereo-LIF, embankment breach instrumentation surveys, three-dimensional partial dam-break measurement reviews, and wave–current seawater-intake PIV.

**Validated ranges.** Reported examples resolve impact-location variation near 0.5% of wave height—larger than a typical pressure-sensor diameter—with about 25% pressure consequence; methods span instantaneous wave/current fields, high-speed impact, repeated bed maps, three-dimensional breach morphology and coupled wave–current intake flow.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances use stereo-planar fluorescence to expose sub-probe impact variability, hybrid imaging/ranging for three-dimensional breaches, custom fluorescent tracers, acoustic map velocimetry, image-based morphology, and combined PIV–CFD classification of wave–current structure flow.

**Disagreements.** Point sensors may have adequate temporal but inadequate spatial resolution; imaging is spatially rich but fails under occlusion, aeration or poor seeding; acoustic methods penetrate turbidity but depend on scattering assumptions; numerical agreement cannot replace calibration; and inferred flux compounds errors from geometry and velocity.

**Limitations.** The coastal-specific indexed evidence is sparse; transparent flumes, controlled lighting and seeded flows simplify reality; air bubbles and sediment obscure optics; acoustic backscatter is concentration- and grain-dependent; probes disturb flow; impact bandwidth and sensor area filter peaks; uncertainty reporting is inconsistent.

**Open questions.** Needs include volumetric free-surface/velocity imaging, robust measurements in aerated and sediment-laden breakers, high-bandwidth distributed pressure, mobile-bed co-registration, nonintrusive force inference, sensor-fusion uncertainty, automated calibration/QA, facility-scale metadata and reproducible raw-data standards.

**Seminal papers.** The branch connects established point and optical ocean sensors to PIV/PTV, acoustic mapping and Doppler methods, photogrammetry, stereo fluorescence for impact surfaces, integrated breach instrumentation, and synchronized experiment–CFD validation.

## Claims

- **C839.** Optical inversion using radiance, absorption, scattering, fluorescence, flow cytometry, and spectroscopy can recover diverse physical and biogeochemical water properties, but specificity depends on calibration and material isolation. *Regime: Optical tools for ocean monitoring and research.* [literature_review_statement, review] (C. Moore 2009, [doi:10.5194/os-5-661-2009](https://doi.org/10.5194/os-5-661-2009))
- **C840.** Acoustic Mapping Velocimetry combines repeated multibeam bed maps with image-velocimetry algorithms and Exner continuity to estimate spatially distributed multidirectional bedload. *Regime: Acoustic mapping velocimetry.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Marian Muste 2016, [doi:10.1002/2015wr018354](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015wr018354))
- **C841.** Stereo-planar laser-induced fluorescence resolved impacting-wave tip variation smaller than ordinary probe spacing: location shifted by about 0.5% of wave height and caused roughly 25% pressure variation. *Regime: Experimental investigation of wave tip variability of impacting waves.* [direct_finding, experimental] (M. van Meerkerk 2020, [doi:10.1063/5.0016467](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0016467))
- **C842.** Breaching experiments require coordinated morphology, depth, velocity, discharge, and pore-pressure measurements using photography/photogrammetry, lasers, PIV/PTV, acoustic, radar, and electronic sensors. *Regime: Overtopping-Induced Embankment Breaching Experiments: State-of-the-Art Review on Measurement and Instrumentation.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Filmon Tquabo Gebremariam 2025, [doi:10.3390/w17071051](https://doi.org/10.3390/w17071051))
- **C843.** Three-dimensional partial dam-break experiments benefit from hybrid PTV and ultrasonic surface measurements because no single technique captures breach, wave, and urban flow at adequate resolution. *Regime: Experimental Modeling of Three-Dimensional (3D) Partial Dam-Break Flows: A Review.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Chuke Meng 2025, [doi:10.3390/w17182792](https://doi.org/10.3390/w17182792))
- **C844.** PIV and numerical modelling jointly resolved wave–current flow around seawater intakes, including geometry-dependent contractions and instability that sharpened with Keulegan–Carpenter number. *Regime: Hydrodynamic Performance of Seawater Intake Structures Through Numerical Modelling and Particle Image Velocimetry.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Mahmood Rahmani Firozjaei 2025, [doi:10.3390/w17172607](https://doi.org/10.3390/w17172607))
- **C1494.** Flume comparisons against laser Doppler velocimetry quantify acoustic Doppler velocimeter accuracy and noise for turbulence measurements. *Regime: Evaluation of the Acoustic Doppler Velocimeter (ADV) for Turbulence Measurements*.* [direct_finding, experimental] (G. Voulgaris 1998, [doi:10.1175/1520-0426(1998)015<0272:eotadv>2.0.co;2](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0426(1998)015<0272:eotadv>2.0.co;2))
