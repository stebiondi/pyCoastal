# Floating structures

`wave_structure.floating` | Coupled wave and body motion.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Floating-structure response couples radiation and diffraction, hydrostatic and Froude–Krylov forcing, viscous drag, rigid-body or hydroelastic motion, and restraints from moorings, joints, PTO, neighboring bodies, and control.

**Governing physics.** Natural-period proximity, parametric resonance, breaking-wave impulses, second-order drift, narrow-gap resonance, porous damping, array radiation, nonlinear wetted area, flexibility, and cable or hinge dynamics can each govern motion or load.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Key controls include wave steepness, wavelength/body size, draft/depth, Froude and Keulegan–Carpenter numbers, gap/beam, submergence, buoyancy/weight, wave-to-natural period, damping ratio, array spacing, porosity, and mooring angle/stiffness.

**Major equations.** Core formulations include six-degree rigid-body equations with added mass, radiation damping and restoring, Cummins/time-domain convolution, diffraction–radiation potential flow, Morison drag, nonlinear Froude–Krylov pressure, RANS/VOF or SPH, cable dynamics, and hydroelastic plate equations.

**Typical methods.** Methods span wave-basin and flume tests, image motion tracking and PIV, load cells and wave arrays, linear and weakly nonlinear potential flow, BEM, OpenFOAM RANS–VOF, DualSPHysics, overset CFD, cable finite elements, surrogate or reduced models, and blind benchmarks.

**Numerical models.** Validated tools include OpenFOAM CFD–motion, DualSPHysics, AQWA, WEC-Sim with CFD-informed viscosity, iterative porous BEM, overset Navier–Stokes, nonlinear Froude–Krylov solvers, hydroelastic plate models, and coupled cable/joint dynamics.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes arrays up to 25 point absorbers, blind focused-wave tests of two taut-moored bodies, 1:4.5 floating-solar rafts, submerged-tunnel tests, porous cylinders, gap-resonance PIV, extreme-wave point absorbers, and field-reconstructed WEC response.

**Validated ranges.** Quantitative findings include 18.1% array wave-height reduction, 53% parametric-resonance power loss, 500-wave extreme simulations, four-order speedup for analytical nonlinear forcing, 44% greater pontoon-breakwater attenuation, and over 70% hybrid reduction of floating-wind motion.

**Recent advances.** Recent work integrates validated SPH for survivability, CFD-informed reduced models, nonlinear cable/joint coupling, floating-solar experiments, porous motion damping, resonance-resolved PIV, hybrid wind–wave attenuation, and performance-oriented floating breakwaters.

**Disagreements.** Linear models can predict heave adequately in some focused-wave tests yet miss other motions and loads; high-fidelity solvers generally improve response but disagree among themselves; body interactions may be negligible in one case but dominate arrays, gaps, and modular systems.

**Limitations.** Full-scale extreme records are rare; viscous, breaking, slamming, mooring, joint, and hydroelastic effects remain model-sensitive; laboratory scaling and idealized seas constrain transfer; and high-fidelity simulation is too expensive for lifetime design.

**Open questions.** Needs include efficient nonlinear long-duration models, standardized blind validation, breaking-wave and impact response, failure-capable hydroelasticity, multidirectional arrays, dynamic cable/contact, coupled control, fatigue, reliability, and uncertainty-aware operability.

**Seminal papers.** The branch connects classical radiation–diffraction and rigid-body response to coupled mooring solvers, array experiments, nonlinear instantaneous-wetted forcing, hydroelastic very-flexible plates, and blind multi-fidelity validation.
