# Estuaries and tidal inlets

`estuaries` | Hydrodynamics, mixing, and exchange in estuaries and inlets.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Estuaries exchange freshwater, salt, sediment and tracers under interacting river, tidal and density forcing. Stratification and mixing set salinity intrusion; gravitational circulation, tidal pumping and straining control exchange, while geometry and adjacent-shelf variability can reorganize these balances.

**Governing physics.** Longitudinal density gradients drive baroclinic exchange, tides advect and mix salinity fronts, and river discharge opposes landward salt transport. Wind and coastal-ocean density anomalies alter boundary conditions; convergence, rotation and evolving bathymetry modify residual circulation and sediment pathways.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include tidal-excursion-to-intrusion-length, estuarine Richardson, Froude and Simpson numbers, freshwater and tidal-volume ratios, mixing and dispersion parameters, convergence length, stratification, Rossby number, settling and Shields measures.

**Major equations.** Models solve hydrostatic mass, momentum, salt and sediment conservation with turbulence closure and free-surface forcing. Knudsen bulk relations and isohaline coordinates diagnose exchange; analytical intrusion laws balance advection and dispersion, and morphodynamic systems add bed evolution.

**Typical methods.** Studies combine salinity, velocity and water-level moorings, hydrographic transects, shelf observations and water sampling with unstructured-grid circulation, HEC-RAS, analytical intrusion, isohaline-budget, coupled groundwater and three-dimensional morphodynamic models.

**Numerical models.** Reviewed systems include validated unstructured hydrodynamics, isohaline-coordinate diagnostics, Savenije intrusion theory, HEC-RAS salinity transport, coupled surface–subsurface variable-density flow and COAWST-like three-dimensional sediment morphodynamics.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes the Merrimack and Hudson contrast, low-flow Willapa Bay, the 2014 Changjiang intrusion, 2015 Sebou supermoon observations, lower Meghna irrigation sampling, idealized funnel estuaries and marsh–farmland surge scenarios.

**Validated ranges.** Examples include 17.6 m/s Changjiang wind and 23-day intake interruption, near-10 cm/s Willapa intrusion, 1.3 dS/m lower-Meghna salinity, and conditional agricultural protection by 200–400 m marsh migration under at least 3 m surge on a 0.1% slope.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances use isohaline coordinates across contrasting regimes, resolve episodic wind-driven intrusion, couple surface and subsurface salinization, embed baroclinicity in long-term morphology and quantify counterintuitive convergence–mixing interactions.

**Disagreements.** Classical steady gravitational-circulation scaling works in long estuaries but can fail in short tidally dominated systems. Convergence may enhance residual flow under constant viscosity yet weaken or reverse tidal-straining circulation when mixing varies over the tide.

**Limitations.** Sparse vertical and lateral sampling, uncertain bathymetry and diffusivity, rapidly changing discharge and coastal boundaries, unresolved turbulence, wetland and groundwater coupling, morphology feedback and limited extreme-event records constrain transfer.

**Open questions.** Priorities include compound drought–surge–wind intrusion, shelf–estuary exchange, wetland migration, oxygen and sediment feedbacks, data assimilation, climate-driven regime shifts and scalable predictors spanning short and long estuaries.

**Seminal papers.** Classical salt-balance, Knudsen and gravitational-circulation theories established exchange and intrusion scaling; later tidal-straining, pumping and estuarine-classification frameworks explained time-dependent mixing and residual transport.

## Claims

- **C366.** For the validated 2006-2012 Delaware Bay hindcast, shelf waves were predominantly remote swell, bathymetric and tidal-current refraction controlled entrance energy, and energetic in-bay waves were mainly locally wind driven with fetch set by wind direction. *Regime: Delaware Bay and adjacent shelf, 2006-2012, validated at three buoys..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Tobias Kukulka 2017, [doi:10.1002/2017jc013370](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017jc013370))
- **C1761.** Mangrove vegetation alters lagoon-wide tidal propagation rather than only local forest flow: vegetation drag preferentially attenuates semidiurnal constituents, enhances the M4 overtide and tidal asymmetry, and produces stronger and longer ebb flow, especially during spring tides. *Regime: Microtidal, shallow Somone Coastal Lagoon, Senegal, during two lunar months in the 2020 dry season, with negligible river discharge and both creek and sheet flow through mangroves..* [direct_finding, mixed] (Marcellin Samou Seujip 2024, [doi:10.1007/s12665-023-11349-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12665-023-11349-5))
