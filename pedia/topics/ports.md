## Synthesis

**Well established.** Port performance couples wave agitation, currents and water levels with vessel motion, navigation clearance, mooring response and sediment exchange. Breakwaters and dredged geometry alter resonance, circulation and transport, so operational and maintenance outcomes cannot be inferred from offshore conditions alone.

**Governing physics.** Incident and reflected waves excite harbor modes and moored vessels; wind, tide and density-driven circulation exchange water and sediment through entrances. Ship squat, heel and wave response consume under-keel clearance, while settling, resuspension, flocculation and near-bed exchange govern siltation.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include relative entrance width and basin length, wavelength-to-basin scale, reflection and amplification coefficients, wave steepness, vessel draft-to-depth and blockage ratios, under-keel-clearance fraction, mooring stiffness and damping, Shields stress, settling-to-friction-velocity ratio and tidal asymmetry.

**Major equations.** Port studies combine spectral or time-domain wave equations, shallow-water and circulation momentum balances, rigid-body vessel and mooring equations, under-keel-clearance budgets, sediment advection–diffusion and bed exchange, and operability or downtime exceedance calculations.

**Typical methods.** Methods include offshore-to-berth spectral transformation, physical harbor models, nested wave and circulation models, AIS and accident analysis, ship-motion and mooring simulation, field monitoring, probabilistic operability assessment, sediment tracers, turbidity surveys and morphodynamic calibration.

**Numerical models.** Model chains couple regional wave forecasts to harbor agitation, vessel response and operational thresholds; navigation tools combine water level, squat, heel and waves. Sedimentation systems couple hydrodynamics, suspended transport, bed exchange, dredging and morphology.

**Experimental datasets.** Reviewed evidence spans irregular-wave harbor models, forty-year spectral climates, Jeju mooring assessment, dock-specific vessel monitoring, dynamic under-keel-clearance studies, Gijón climate projections and multi-season Zeebrugge mud observations and models.

**Validated ranges.** Examples include 81.6% multimodal sea states at a near-port point, reconstruction correlations near 0.995–1.000 for aggregated wave parameters, spring mud import two-to-four times neap values, and source-specific vessel-response prediction errors below operationally evaluated limits.

**Recent advances.** Recent work clusters full directional spectra, learns vessel response from field data, delivers multi-port forecasts, integrates AIS risk evidence, resolves ship-specific access probabilities and uses multiple observational constraints to expose sediment-model nonuniqueness.

**Disagreements.** Aggregate significant wave height can hide frequency-direction combinations that cause different in-port response. Sediment models may fit available observations yet predict opposite long-term convergence, and generic vessel thresholds can conflict with dock-specific monitored limits.

**Limitations.** Sparse entrance and berth observations, uncertain reflection and infragravity forcing, nonlinear vessel and fender behavior, changing bathymetry, human factors, climate-model spread, cohesive-bed exchange and dredging records limit transferable operational prediction.

**Open questions.** Priorities include seamless offshore-to-vessel forecasts, infragravity and resonance prediction, autonomous and climate-resilient navigation, multi-vessel interaction, data-assimilative sediment budgets, beneficial sediment management and uncertainty-aware downtime planning.

**Seminal papers.** Classical harbor resonance and diffraction studies established basin agitation analysis; ship maneuvering and under-keel-clearance work linked hydrodynamics to navigation, while estuarine sediment theory established tidal pumping and exchange frameworks for siltation.

## Claims

- **C1477.** The SAMOA operational service delivers high-resolution meteorological and ocean forecasts tailored to safety, environmental, and operational decisions in Spanish ports. *Regime: Coastal ocean forecasting in Spanish ports: the SAMOA operational service.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Marcos García Sotillo 2019, [doi:10.1080/1755876x.2019.1606765](https://doi.org/10.1080/1755876x.2019.1606765))
- **C1479.** Field surveys of a sub-Arctic industrial harbor relate benthic communities to sediment properties and heavy-metal gradients for cumulative-impact assessment. *Regime: Biodiversity and Habitat Assessment of Coastal Benthic Communities in a Sub-Arctic Industrial Harbor Area.* [direct_finding, field] (Elliot Dreujou 2020, [doi:10.3390/w12092424](https://doi.org/10.3390/w12092424))
- **C1495.** A technical review categorizes greenhouse-gas and pollutant-emission sources in ports and assesses mitigation and monitoring approaches. *Regime: Pollutant Emissions in Ports: A Comprehensive Review.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Salvatore Barberi 2021, [doi:10.3390/infrastructures6080114](https://doi.org/10.3390/infrastructures6080114))
- **C1617.** Fully nonlinear FUNWAVE simulations show that Bragg-resonant bar fields can mitigate regular- and group-wave harbor oscillations; long bars reduced modeled nonlinear resonance by up to 76.4%, versus 23.0% for short bars, with stronger mitigation as bar number or amplitude increased. *Regime: Elongated constant-depth harbor with external sinusoidal bars and the regular/group-wave and bar parameter ranges tested..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Junliang Gao 2021, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2021.103977](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2021.103977))
- **C1620.** Fully nonlinear FUNWAVE simulations show that focused transient wave groups can directly excite harbor resonance when their spectral peak approaches a harbor mode, while nonlinear interactions can additionally excite lower modes; runup and resonance increase with spectral width and are strongest for normal incidence. *Regime: Four elongated constant-depth harbors, focused groups at 0.50 Hz, and tested amplitude, bandwidth and incident-direction ranges..* [direct_finding, numerical] (Junliang Gao 2020, [doi:10.1016/j.coastaleng.2020.103670](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2020.103670))
