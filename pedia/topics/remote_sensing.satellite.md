## Synthesis

**Well established.** Satellite radar altimetry provides long, repeatable observations of sea surface height, significant wave height, and wind speed that complement sparse in situ coastal networks, but quality generally deteriorates close to land and sampling is not continuous.

**Governing physics.** Radar range and returned-waveform shape determine sea surface height and sea-state estimates; land contamination, surface roughness, atmosphere and geophysical corrections, track geometry, tides, and land motion all affect coastal interpretation.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Correlation, normalized scatter, detection rate, and regression slope quantify skill but are dataset-specific statistical measures. No universal dimensionless parameter removes the effects of coast distance, waveform contamination, or sampling geometry.

**Major equations.** Core calculations combine corrected radar range with satellite orbit height, derive wave and wind metrics from waveforms/backscatter, difference relative tide-gauge and absolute altimeter trends for vertical land motion, and use correlation or error metrics to rank products and tide models.

**Typical methods.** Typical workflows collocate altimeters with buoys or tide gauges, merge multiple missions, retrack coastal waveforms, apply objective mapping, combine optical wetting indices with tide predictions, fuse numerical simulations with machine learning, and validate against independent fixed instruments.

**Numerical models.** Relevant models include multi-mission objective analysis, geophysical correction and retracking chains, random-forest event classifiers, global ocean tide models, and the eo-tides locally adaptive ensemble.

**Experimental datasets.** Reviewed datasets include 36 months of Iberian radar-buoy-Sentinel observations, five-mission northeastern US metocean composites, 27 years at 50 Southeast Asian VLM sites, a ten-year Gulf of Lion simulation/altimetry record, and continent-wide Australian tide-model rankings every 10 km.

**Validated ranges.** Skill is product and regime bound: Iberian Sentinel-3 SWH validation achieved R above 0.94 and RMSE 0.17-0.42 m; northeastern US composites are climatological and weaken near land; Australian NDWI rankings sample tidal phase more directly than amplitude.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances combine sensors and models rather than relying on one product: tide gauges plus altimetry recover VLM, optical NDWI plus altimetry guides tide ensembles, and simulation-altimetry-ADCP fusion enables event classification.

**Disagreements.** Altimetry can agree closely with buoys in favorable coastal collocations yet still miss extremes or become biased near shore. This is a resolution, sampling, and waveform-regime difference rather than conflicting evidence about universal sensor accuracy.

**Limitations.** Land-contaminated waveforms, sparse repeat tracks, asynchronous sampling, correction uncertainty, sun-synchronous tidal aliasing, fixed-sensor coverage, and site-trained machine learning constrain transferability and event detection.

**Open questions.** Priorities include high-resolution coastal retracking, uncertainty-aware multi-mission fusion, event-scale sampling, joint absolute and relative sea-level attribution, transferable current classifiers, and exploitation of SWOT and future missions.

**Seminal papers.** The 2023 altimetry review is the synthesis anchor in this screened slice, documenting the multi-decadal mission lineage and transition from open-ocean measurement toward operational coastal applications.
