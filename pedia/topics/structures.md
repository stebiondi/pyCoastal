## Synthesis

**Well established.** Coastal structures modify waves, currents, water levels, sediment pathways and access while resisting hydraulic, geotechnical and environmental actions; performance therefore depends on coupled load, response, foundation, material, morphology and service-limit states over the lifecycle.

**Governing physics.** Incident and reflected waves, breaking, impact, overtopping, currents, uplift and tsunami generate pressures and forces; geometry transforms flow; armor, concrete, steel, foundations and joints resist through mass, strength, friction and stiffness while scour, settlement, fatigue and corrosion reduce capacity.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include relative freeboard and depth, wave steepness and surf similarity, reflection/transmission/overtopping coefficients, stability number, relative armor density, permeability, slope, breaker and impact parameters, Keulegan–Carpenter and diffraction ratios, safety/reliability indices and damage/deterioration ratios.

**Major equations.** Core formulations include wave transformation and overtopping relations, pressure/force and Morison-type loading, armor stability numbers, sliding/overturning/bearing/uplift limit states, porous flow, soil–structure interaction, scour, fatigue and corrosion deterioration, structural dynamics and reliability or lifecycle risk.

**Typical methods.** Methods combine design guidance, physical modelling, CFD/SPH and structural/geotechnical analysis, prototype monitoring, post-event forensics, material and durability tests, limit-state and reliability assessment, lifecycle inspection, retrofit trials and system/service risk analysis.

**Numerical models.** Tools include spectral and phase-resolving waves, CFD/VOF and SPH, porous-flow and overtopping models, finite-element structural and soil analysis, scour and morphology models, data-driven hydraulic emulators, deterioration, reliability and network consequence models.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes vertical-breakwater overtopping, composite seawall crown-wall tests, recurve retrofits, coral-sand revetment foundations, stepped-revetment cases, cross-geometry simulations, coastal high-rise integrity and concrete/climate-resilience reviews.

**Validated ranges.** The reviewed evidence spans vertical and composite seawalls, stepped and armor revetments, breakwaters, coastal buildings and infrastructure under waves, overtopping, aggressive exposure and future sea levels; each transfers only within tested geometry, foundation, material and forcing.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances include high-resolution impact and overtopping experiments, CFD/SPH, data-driven emulators, recurve and crown-wall retrofits, monitoring-informed condition assessment, climate-adjusted loads, durable low-carbon materials and infrastructure-network risk.

**Disagreements.** Reducing overtopping can increase local loads or reflected energy; rigid protection can transfer erosion; hydraulic model accuracy does not prove structural or foundation safety; data-driven formula improvement may not extrapolate; least-cost robust mass can conflict with adaptability, carbon and ecology.

**Limitations.** Scale effects distort impact air, viscosity, permeability and sediment; foundations and scour are often simplified; joint failure and construction variability are sparse; deterioration and hidden defects are poorly observed; load combinations and cascading service loss remain uncertain under nonstationary climate.

**Open questions.** Priorities include coupled hydraulic–structural–geotechnical failure, compound hazards and debris, breaking-impact aeration, scour and morphology feedback, aging and inspection, low-carbon durable materials, adaptive retrofits, nature–structure interaction and system-level reliability.

**Seminal papers.** Classical breakwater and seawall research established armor stability, overtopping, reflection/transmission, wave pressures and sliding/overturning checks; later reliability, soil interaction, numerical fluid–structure modelling and lifecycle durability broadened performance assessment.

## Claims

- **C1049.** Machine-learning enhancement of vertical-breakwater overtopping prediction shows that data-driven tools can supplement hydraulic formulae when training range, geometry and validation limits are explicit. *Regime: Enhanced wave overtopping simulation at vertical breakwaters using machine learning algorithms.* [direct_finding, numerical] (M. A. Habib 2023, [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0289318](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0289318))
- **C1050.** A review of climate risks to coastal critical infrastructure links direct loading, flooding, erosion, access loss, service interdependence and adaptation across structural systems. *Regime: Risks to Coastal Critical Infrastructure from Climate Change.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Indrajit Pal 2023, [doi:10.1146/annurev-environ-112320-101903](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-environ-112320-101903))
- **C1051.** A stepped-concrete revetment case demonstrates how structural layout, toe and slope conditions, wave exposure, construction practice and observed shoreline response jointly determine protection performance. *Regime: COASTAL PROTECTION BY A STEPPED CONCRETE REVETMENT IN THAILAND.* [direct_finding, mixed] (SARINYA SANITWONG-NA-AYUTTHAYA 2022, [doi:10.46754/jssm.2022.07.003](https://doi.org/10.46754/jssm.2022.07.003))
- **C1052.** Parametric physical tests of crown-wall modifications quantify how a composite seawall can be adapted to future sea levels while retaining structure-specific overtopping constraints. *Regime: Crown Wall Modifications as Response to Wave Overtopping under a Future Sea Level Scenario: An Experimental Parametric Study for an Innovative Composite Seawall.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Pasquale Contestabile 2020, [doi:10.3390/app10072227](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10072227))
- **C1053.** Physical-model evidence shows that recurve retrofits can reduce overtopping at vertical seawalls, with performance governed by recurve geometry and incident hydraulic conditions. *Regime: Improved prediction of wave overtopping rates at vertical seawalls with recurve retrofitting.* [direct_finding, experimental] (S. Dong 2024, [doi:10.1016/j.oceaneng.2024.117647](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oceaneng.2024.117647))
- **C1054.** SPH simulation across several coastal-structure geometries demonstrates a common numerical framework for comparing free-surface overtopping processes and structure-specific response. *Regime: Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics Simulation of Wave Overtopping Characteristics for Different Coastal Structures.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Jaan H. Pu 2012, [doi:10.1100/2012/163613](https://doi.org/10.1100/2012/163613))
- **C1055.** Regular-wave physical modelling of a revetment breakwater on reclaimed coral-calcareous sand links foundation conditions, armor response and hydraulic loading to structural stability. *Regime: Physical Modeling of the Stability of a Revetment Breakwater Built on Reclaimed Coral Calcareous Sand Foundation in the South China Sea—Regular Wave.* [direct_finding, experimental] (He 2021, [doi:10.3390/app11052325](https://doi.org/10.3390/app11052325))
- **C1056.** A review of high-rise structures in coastal regions integrates wind, salt exposure, flooding, foundations, materials, deterioration and structural-integrity concerns. *Regime: Review of Structural Integrity Concerns in High-rise Buildings in Coastal Regions.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Aduwo Egidario Bridgette 2024, [doi:10.13189/cea.2024.120523](https://doi.org/10.13189/cea.2024.120523))
- **C1057.** A coastal-resilience review frames durable and lower-impact concrete materials as part of structural adaptation to sea-level rise, flooding, aggressive exposure and extreme loading. *Regime: Developing Coastal Resilience to Climate Change in Panama Through Sustainable Concrete Applications.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Kathleen J. Castillo-Martínez 2025, [doi:10.3390/jcs9110575](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcs9110575))
