## Synthesis

**Well established.** Extreme wave climate is inferred from block maxima or threshold exceedances after accounting for storm dependence; return levels depend on record length, threshold, declustering, spatial pooling, covariates, and whether the target is sea-state, individual-wave, or joint coastal conditions.

**Governing physics.** Extreme waves reflect storm intensity, translation, track, fetch and duration, tropical-cyclone structure, climate-mode modulation, directional exposure, bathymetric transformation, and joint sea-level state; statistical tails encode these generating processes imperfectly.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Relevant controls include exceedance probability and return period, threshold quantile, extremal index and declustering interval, shape parameter, normalized return level, storm duration and footprint, wave steepness, directional sector, water depth/wavelength, climate index, and ensemble spread.

**Major equations.** Methods use GEV block maxima, generalized Pareto peaks over threshold, Poisson occurrence, return-level inversion, covariate-varying location/scale/shape, mixture and weather-type convolution, regional frequency/index-flood concepts, copulas or joint simulators, bootstrap tests, and Bayesian or penalized likelihood.

**Typical methods.** Typical workflows merge buoy and satellite observations, hindcast/reanalysis, ensemble forecasts, synthetic cyclone catalogs, storm tracking and clustering, POT or annual maxima, stationary/nonstationary comparison, weather types, regionalization, multivariate dependence, nested wave downscaling, and climate ensembles.

**Numerical models.** Models include WAVEWATCH III, SWAN, SWAN–ADCIRC, COAWST nesting, ERA5 and other reanalyses, ECMWF ensemble forecasts, parametric tropical-cyclone waves, climate emulators, hybrid statistical–dynamical downscaling, weather-type mixtures, penalized nonstationary POT, and joint stochastic simulators.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes 30-year global satellite records, 39-year Red Sea and 45-year southwest Pacific hindcasts, 240-hour ECMWF archives equivalent to long samples, ERA5 offshore lease areas, a 1000-year synthetic cyclone set, 19 CMIP6 models, and regional Mediterranean, Black Sea, China and East Asia studies.

**Validated ranges.** The corpus spans 30-year satellite POT, 39- and 45-year hindcasts, effectively 226 years from ensemble forecasts, a 1000-year cyclone catalog for 100-year waves, 50-year lease-area design conditions, 99th-percentile East Asian events, and 2070–2100 projections from 19 CMIP6 models.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances exploit long satellite records, synthetic millennial cyclone catalogs, multi-model CMIP6 nesting, recurrence-informed nonstationarity, flexible penalized covariates, climate emulators and multivariate compound extremes, and joint offshore-to-coastal stochastic simulation.

**Disagreements.** Stationary and nonstationary models can give materially different return values, but an imposed trend may be insignificant; wave-, sea-state-, and storm-level independence assumptions alter individual-wave tails; model choice, threshold and covariate flexibility trade bias against variance.

**Limitations.** Instrument and hindcast records remain short for rare tails; satellite sampling is intermittent; reanalysis under-resolves cyclones and coasts; synthetic storms depend on generators; climate ensembles and downscaling introduce cascaded uncertainty; compound dependence is data-hungry.

**Open questions.** Priorities include nonstationary tail validation, changing cyclone frequency and track, spatially coherent storm footprints, directional and seasonal dependence, individual-wave extremes inside changing sea states, compound wave–level tails, model averaging, and decision-making under deep uncertainty.

**Seminal papers.** The branch progresses from stationary annual maxima and POT toward weather-type mixtures, regional storm-footprint pooling, ensemble-forecast pseudo-climates, covariate-dependent and penalized tails, joint stochastic offshore–coastal simulation, and climate-conditioned projections.
