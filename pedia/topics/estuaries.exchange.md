# Estuarine exchange flow

`estuaries.exchange` | Net exchange and flushing.

Parent: [Estuaries and tidal inlets](estuaries.md)

Papers: 37. Claims: 33. Equations: 0.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Estuarine exchange is compensating inflow and outflow driven by baroclinic pressure but strongly modified by tides, mixing, wind, river discharge, lateral circulation, geometry and shelf conditions; net volume transport alone conceals this renewal.

**Governing physics.** Density gradients drive gravitational circulation; tidal straining and periodic stratification modulate shear; covariance of eddy viscosity and shear rectifies tides; lateral advection and bathymetry redistribute exchange; wind can reinforce, weaken or reverse flow; mixing entrains salt and transforms water masses.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include estuarine Froude and Richardson numbers, tidal and freshwater velocity ratio, Simpson and Wedderburn-type straining measures, salinity difference, mixing efficiency, depth/width and convergence, tidal excursion/length, river flow/tidal prism, wind stress/baroclinic pressure, and flushing-time/tidal-period.

**Major equations.** Core formulations include along-channel momentum and salt balance, Knudsen relations, residual/tidal decomposition, exchange in salinity coordinates (TEF), salinity variance and mixing budgets, eddy-viscosity–shear covariance, box/flushing models, water-age transport, and nondimensional estuarine scaling.

**Typical methods.** Methods combine moored velocity/salinity sections, ship surveys and tracers, residual harmonic and covariance analysis, primitive-equation models, idealized channels, TEF salinity-coordinate integration, variance/mixing budgets, particle or age tracers, flushing boxes, sensitivity experiments, and shelf-source tracking.

**Numerical models.** Models include idealized 1DV/2DV and three-dimensional circulation systems, Hudson and urban-channel configurations, TEF diagnostics, salinity-variance budgets, eddy-viscosity–shear covariance, unsteady flushing models, shelf-source particle tracking, plume/coastal forcing experiments, and tracer budgets.

**Experimental datasets.** The corpus spans Hudson and San Francisco systems, Galveston and Capibaribe bays, Wadden and German Bight estuaries, macrotidal and narrow idealized channels, along-coast coupled estuaries, glacial fjords, and the segmented multi-basin Salish Sea.

**Validated ranges.** Evidence covers well-mixed to partially and periodically stratified estuaries, weakly to strongly tidal and macrotidal settings, narrow to wide channels, seasonal segmentation, spring–neap variability, local and remote winds, channel deepening, glacial plume forcing, and connected shelf–estuary systems.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances use salinity-coordinate TEF, variance budgets tying exchange to mixing, shelf-source tracing, segmented-estuary diagnostics, plume-versus-coastal attribution in fjords, multiple closed tracer budgets, and explicit tests of remote-ocean forcing limits.

**Disagreements.** Classical two-layer gravitational circulation is useful but incomplete: tidal rectification can generate exchange in well-mixed water, lateral advection can rival longitudinal dynamics, wind can reverse the expected pattern, and flushing/residence estimates depend on diagnostic definition and temporal averaging.

**Limitations.** Sparse sections miss lateral recirculation; residuals depend on averaging; salinity-coordinate transport can hide geography; model mixing is resolution/closure sensitive; residence time is not unique; open-boundary shelf properties and episodic winds are uncertain; constituent transformation complicates tracer closure.

**Open questions.** Needs include unified TEF and water-age diagnostics, lateral and multi-inlet exchange, event-to-climate variability, morphology/deepening and sea-level effects, compound wind–river–shelf forcing, exchange–biogeochemistry coupling, and observation-based mixing closure.

**Seminal papers.** The branch evolves from partially mixed momentum/salt balances and tidal rectification toward lateral-advection models, mixing covariance, wind reversal, salinity-variance links, TEF, shelf-source attribution, seasonal segmentation, and multi-tracer budgets.
