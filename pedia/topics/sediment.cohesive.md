# Cohesive sediment

`sediment.cohesive` | Mud erosion, flocculation, and deposition.

Parent: [Sediment transport](sediment.md)

Papers: 27. Claims: 25. Equations: 0.

## Synthesis

**Well established.** Cohesive sediment aggregates, settles, deposits, consolidates, erodes, and resuspends through history-dependent interactions among clay mineralogy, organic matter, salinity, concentration, turbulence, waves, currents, stratification, and bed state.

**Governing physics.** Key processes are collision and aggregation, breakup, effective-density and shape change, free and hindered settling, flocculation by salt and polymers, erosion threshold and rate, simultaneous deposition, consolidation, biodiffusion, fluid mud, and tidal convergence.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Important controls include bed shear relative to critical stress, settling-to-mixing timescale, floc and primary-particle size, effective density, solids concentration, salinity, organic/cohesive fraction, dry bulk density, Rouse-type suspension, and tidal/wave transport asymmetry.

**Major equations.** Frameworks include Partheniades erosion and Krone deposition, aggregation-breakup population balances, hindered settling, advection-diffusion-settling, bed layering and consolidation, entrainment-flux inversion, wave-boundary-layer fluid mud, and mixed sand-mud morphology.

**Typical methods.** Methods span settling columns, erosion devices, floc cameras and holography, field turbidity and tripod arrays, sediment cores, salinity/EPS experiments, mass-balance inversion, particle tracking, grain-resolved simulation, and coupled 3-D sediment-morphology models.

**Numerical models.** ROMS/COAWST, Delft3D, SUNTANS, CSTMS, particle tracking, population-style floc modules, and grain-resolved immersed-boundary models represent different scales. Spatially variable erosion and multiple floc classes can be essential for field skill.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes Rhine-ROFI particle imaging, Venice Lagoon sensor inversion, four-site San Francisco Bay SSC, Seine and Chesapeake hindcasts, Hengsha 11-day bed records, salinity experiments, sand-clay columns, and consolidated-bed threshold tests.

**Validated ranges.** Reported findings include wave contribution to 44% of spring ETM mass, 25% oxygen decrease and 50% ammonium increase, salinity effects from 0.5 ppt saturating near 3-5 ppt, settling 0.1-10 mm/s, over 4 cm shelf deposition, and 38 mm erosion followed by 68 mm accretion.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances directly image composition-dependent flocs, quantify low-salinity thresholds, infer erodibility from routine time series, model wave-supported fluid mud, resolve grain-scale cohesion, and couple sediment resuspension to oxygen and nutrients.

**Disagreements.** Salt is not universally required to form flocs because organic-rich freshwater mud already aggregates, yet salt accelerates growth and binds residual fines. Bentonite showed a sharper 0.6-1.0 PSU threshold, demonstrating mineral- and organic-specific rather than universal salinity response.

**Limitations.** Fixed settling speeds and thresholds, sparse floc-density and bed-strength observations, instrument disruption, mineral specificity, uncertain biological cohesion, fluid-mud rheology, mixed-bed segregation, scale gaps, and calibration equifinality constrain prediction.

**Open questions.** Needs include coupled mineral-organic floc dynamics, history-dependent erosion and consolidation, simultaneous erosion/deposition, fluid-mud turbulence damping, biostabilization/bioturbation, mixed-bed stratigraphy, and morphology-biogeochemistry feedback.

**Seminal papers.** The branch builds on Partheniades-Krone erosion/deposition, hindered settling, estuarine trapping and tidal pumping, extended by floc population dynamics, mixed-bed layering, fluid mud, remote erodibility inversion, and grain-resolved cohesion.

## Claims

- **C509.** Estuarine turbidity maxima reflect longitudinal convergence of tidally averaged suspended-particle transport produced by multiple, system-dependent hydrodynamic and sediment processes. *Regime: estuaries across tidal, river, stratification, and sediment regimes.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Hans Burchard 2017, [doi:10.1146/annurev-marine-010816-060535](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-marine-010816-060535))
- **C510.** Long-term mixed-sediment modelling showed that mud supply narrows and stabilizes estuaries, with fluvial mud distributed throughout, marine mud concentrated near the mouth, and waves suppressing downstream muddy coastlines. *Regime: idealized convergent estuary across tide, river, waves, and mud supply.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Lisanne Braat 2017, [doi:10.5194/esurf-5-617-2017](https://doi.org/10.5194/esurf-5-617-2017))
- **C511.** In the Seine model, energetic waves supplied up to 44% of maximum spring-tide ETM mass and tripled mean-tide mass relative to calm conditions, while river flow mainly shifted location and spring-neap phasing created hysteresis. *Regime: macrotidal Seine Estuary.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Florent Grasso 2017, [doi:10.1002/2017jc013185](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017jc013185))
- **C512.** ROMS v3.6/COAWST r1234 added cohesive and mixed-sediment algorithms for water-column floc dynamics, bed-floc change, erosion/deposition, biodiffusion, and higher-fidelity bed layering. *Regime: idealized and realistic cohesive and mixed-sediment environments.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Christopher R. Sherwood 2018, [doi:10.5194/gmd-11-1849-2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-11-1849-2018))
- **C513.** Modelled Chesapeake resuspension shifted production and remineralization and, averaged over the channel bottom water, decreased oxygen by about 25% and increased ammonium by about 50%. *Regime: Chesapeake Bay summers 2002 and 2003.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Julia M. Moriarty 2020, [doi:10.1007/s12237-020-00763-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12237-020-00763-8))
- **C514.** A Thermaikos Gulf particle model tracked evolving cohesive-floc properties through stratified advection, turbulent diffusion, settling, coagulation, breakup, consolidation, deposition, and bed erosion. *Regime: Thermaikos Gulf with river, bed, atmospheric, and drainage sources.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Yannis Ν. Krestenitis 2007, [doi:10.5194/os-3-91-2007](https://doi.org/10.5194/os-3-91-2007))
- **C515.** A Venice Lagoon mass-balance inversion used routine wind, tide, wave, and turbidity observations to estimate settling velocity and critical erosion stress without disturbing the biologically active bed. *Regime: shallow wave-dominated Venice Lagoon.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Chiara Venier 2014, [doi:10.1002/2013jf003019](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013jf003019))
- **C516.** In South San Francisco Bay, two floc classes with spatially varying erosion ratios and critical stress reproduced four-site SSC, and shoal export contributed to channel suspension at a magnitude comparable to local resuspension. *Regime: South San Francisco Bay shoals and deep channel.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Yi‐Ju Chou 2018, [doi:10.1029/2017jc013064](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017jc013064))
- **C517.** An entrainment-flux method inferred critical erosion stress and erosion-rate coefficients from tidal SSC time series with favorable agreement to direct Gust-microcosm measurements. *Regime: small narrow tidal estuary.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Rooni Mathew 2017, [doi:10.1007/s10236-017-1055-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10236-017-1055-2))
- **C518.** Natural-mud experiments showed floc growth and equilibrium size increasing with salinity with or without organic matter, with detectable enhancement at 0.5 ppt and only marginal further growth beyond about 3-5 ppt. *Regime: deionized to estuarine salinity with and without natural organic matter.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Ehsan Abolfazli 2023, [doi:10.1029/2022jc019255](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022jc019255))
- **C519.** A settling-velocity review found that concentration, particle size and shape, temperature, salinity, organic matter, and pH interact, while sampling instruments can disrupt flocs and yield inconsistent sizes. *Regime: reservoir and estuarine still-water settling.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Yani Li 2024, [doi:10.3390/w16070938](https://doi.org/10.3390/w16070938))
- **C520.** Rhine-ROFI observations found mineral, mixed aggregate, and organic particle classes with settling velocities of 0.1-10 mm/s and aspect ratios 1-10, while algae altered floc density within minutes. *Regime: Rhine ROFI near bed during neap tide.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Zeinab Safar 2022, [doi:10.3389/fmars.2022.896163](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2022.896163))
- **C521.** Settling-column tests showed distinct sand-clay layers at low clay content, transitional segregation as clay increased, and no clear segregation at high clay content, consistent with polydisperse hindered-settling predictions. *Regime: sand-clay mixtures across fractional composition.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Alan Cuthbertson 2016, [doi:10.1007/s10236-016-0958-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10236-016-0958-7))
- **C522.** Bentonite settling increased sharply across 0.6-1.0 PSU and then plateaued; 60% of aggregates exceeded 20 micrometres, while EPS enlarged flocs but did not dominate settling. *Regime: bentonite from deionized to seawater salinity.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Ellen Krahl 2022, [doi:10.3389/feart.2022.886006](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2022.886006))
- **C523.** Adding wave-supported fluid mud to the Atchafalaya model produced a lutocline, more than 4 cm offshore deposition, and downslope flux comparable to suspended flux, with hindered settling extending transport offshore. *Regime: Atchafalaya shelf during March 2008 cold fronts.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Zhengchen Zang 2020, [doi:10.1029/2019jc015269](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019jc015269))
- **C524.** Grain-resolved immersed-boundary simulations represent van der Waals cohesion and reveal statistical aggregation-breakup equilibrium, dense-suspension settling, and cohesive granular-collapse behavior not yet captured reliably by continuum laws. *Regime: cohesive particles in viscous and turbulent open-water flows.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Bernhard Vowinckel 2023, [doi:10.1017/flo.2023.20](https://doi.org/10.1017/flo.2023.20))
- **C525.** At Hengsha Shoal, a wave-swell event produced 38 mm erosion followed by 68 mm accretion, and a simultaneous erosion-deposition paradigm reproduced cohesive bed change better than exclusive deposition. *Regime: Hengsha Shoal near Yangtze ETM.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Qi Shen 2023, [doi:10.3389/fmars.2022.1045335](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2022.1045335))
- **C526.** Experiments found cohesive-sediment incipient shear stress increasing with consolidation time, dry bulk density, and cohesive fraction, and a formula using relative density and cohesive content matched the data. *Regime: consolidated cohesive sediment mixtures.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Caiwen Shu 2020, [doi:10.1007/s12517-020-05978-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12517-020-05978-4))
- **C1451.** Controlled experiments show that increasing clay content reduces mixed sand-mud bedform dimensions and steepness, limiting cohesionless-sediment predictors. *Regime: Sticky stuff: Redefining bedform prediction in modern and ancient environments.* [direct_finding, experimental] (Robert J. Schindler 2015, [doi:10.1130/g36262.1](https://doi.org/10.1130/g36262.1))
- **C1480.** York River observations show tidal-straining asymmetry in turbulent mixing drives distinct advective and pumping components of fine-sediment flux. *Regime: Sediment pumping by tidal asymmetry in a partially mixed estuary.* [direct_finding, field] (Malcolm E. Scully 2007, [doi:10.1029/2006jc003784](https://doi.org/10.1029/2006jc003784))
- **C1481.** Mudstone microtextures and paleoflow evidence identify storm-generated, wave-enhanced sediment gravity flows dispersing mud across an ancient prodelta. *Regime: Mud dispersal across a Cretaceous prodelta: Storm‐generated, wave‐enhanced sediment gravity flows inferred from mudstone microtexture and microfacies.* [direct_finding, field] (A. Guy Plint 2013, [doi:10.1111/sed.12068](https://doi.org/10.1111/sed.12068))
- **C1482.** Digital inline holography measures in-situ cohesive-floc size and settling velocity, resolving properties that control suspended-sediment settling flux. *Regime: The application of holography to the analysis of size and settling velocity of suspended cohesive sediments.* [direct_finding, experimental] (George W. Graham 2010, [doi:10.4319/lom.2010.8.1](https://doi.org/10.4319/lom.2010.8.1))
- **C1483.** Estuary modeling links channel deepening, tidal amplification, fine-sediment import, and sediment-induced drag reduction in the hyper-turbid lower Ems River. *Regime: Fine sediment transport into the hyper-turbid lower Ems River: the role of channel deepening and sediment-induced drag reduction.* [direct_finding, numerical] (D.S. van Maren 2015, [doi:10.1007/s10236-015-0821-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10236-015-0821-2))
- **C1484.** A coupled nearshore morphodynamic study maps cohesive-coast erosion and deposition around a detached low-crested breakwater. *Regime: Dynamics of Sediment Transport and Erosion-Deposition Patterns in the Locality of a Detached Low-Crested Breakwater on a Cohesive Coast.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Arniza Fitri 2019, [doi:10.3390/w11081721](https://doi.org/10.3390/w11081721))
