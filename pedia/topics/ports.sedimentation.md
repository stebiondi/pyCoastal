## Synthesis

**Well established.** Port and channel sedimentation occurs when imported or locally resuspended material converges, settles, consolidates, or migrates into sheltered basins and maintained depths faster than natural export or dredging removes it.

**Governing physics.** Controls include tidal filling and exchange, entrance gyres and flow separation, waves and wind currents, longshore and fluvial supply, aggregation and settling, salinity and sediment stratification, bed erosion and consolidation, and structure-induced sediment-cell changes.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Relevant groups include settling-to-advection timescale, Rouse- and Shields-type mobility, tidal excursion relative to entrance scale, spring-neap forcing, aggregate-to-primary-particle size, concentration and density stratification, and channel depth relative to ambient morphology.

**Major equations.** Frameworks combine suspended-sediment advection-diffusion-settling, erosion-deposition and consolidation, hydrodynamic momentum, bed evolution, particle or aggregate transport, source mixing, and geometric decomposition of tidal filling and horizontal/vertical exchange.

**Typical methods.** Methods use multilevel turbidity and mud-density observations, bathymetric and dredging records, satellite imagery, calibrated hydrodynamic-sediment models, flux decomposition, core erosion imaging, elemental and isotope provenance, and intervention scenario modelling.

**Numerical models.** Models diagnose entrance recirculation, water-bed exchange alternatives, aggregate transport modes, coupled hydrodynamic-bed change, and structural countermeasures. Equifinality remains possible when different near-bed mechanisms fit the same observations.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes two Belgian harbor-basin exchange datasets, Zeebrugge field and dredging records, Nowshahr field calibration, tidal James River cores, Gladstone source samples, Lekki post-construction operation, and Dinh An channel surveys.

**Validated ranges.** Explicit findings include 50-100 Mt of hypothesized legacy mud, aggregates 50-270 times primary-particle size, spring-tide import 2-4 times neap, and import concentrated from roughly two hours before high water to high water.

**Recent advances.** Recent advances combine isotope fingerprints, aggregate imaging, multi-timescale fluid-mud monitoring, calibrated competing-mechanism models, nature-based sediment accommodation, and scenario optimization for channel stability.

**Disagreements.** At Zeebrugge, an erodible compact bed and reduced water-bed exchange reproduced available data comparably but predicted sediment export versus strengthened convergence. Existing observations therefore do not uniquely identify the maintaining mechanism.

**Limitations.** Sparse near-bed exchange, floc, density, consolidation, and bedload data; uncertain sediment provenance; bathymetric sampling; operational dredging feedback; ship effects; extreme events; and model equifinality constrain forecasts.

**Open questions.** Priorities are continuous mass budgets, fluid-mud rheology, aggregate-resolved transport, dredging and disposal feedback, climate and storm nonstationarity, source apportionment uncertainty, and whole-cell effects of port interventions.

**Seminal papers.** The branch builds from classical tidal-prism, exchange-flow, settling, erosion, and dredging-maintenance concepts toward geometric flux decomposition, cohesive aggregation, provenance tracing, and operational morphodynamic design.

## Claims

- **C470.** At Zeebrugge, two models that fit concentration, satellite, bed-level, mud-content, and dredging data predicted opposite convergence responses, showing that uncertain near-bed exchange obscures whether decades-old port construction and 50-100 Mt of mobilized mud sustain the turbidity maximum. *Regime: Belgian-Dutch coast around Zeebrugge port.* [direct_finding, mixed] (D.S. van Maren 2020, [doi:10.1016/j.margeo.2020.106186](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.margeo.2020.106186))
- **C471.** At Nowshahr, field-validated modelling showed that eastward wind-driven current separates around the western breakwater and forms a recirculation zone that imports resuspended shoreline sediment into the port entrance and access channel. *Regime: Nowshahr port entrance on the southern Caspian Sea under storms.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Ayyuob Mahmoodi 2020, [doi:10.3390/jmse8040284](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse8040284))
- **C472.** The Lekki Sandbar Breakwater deliberately uses heavy updrift accretion to supplement sand construction and pairs it with a replenishable sand engine, maintaining port operation while managing downdrift sediment balance. *Regime: Lekki, Nigeria, on the high-drift Gulf of Guinea coast.* [direct_finding, field] (Bart-Jan van der Spek 2020, [doi:10.3390/w12051446](https://doi.org/10.3390/w12051446))
- **C473.** A geometric decomposition of two Belgian harbor datasets separates tidal filling, horizontal exchange, and vertical exchange, providing a calibration-ready diagnosis of the phasing and spatial correlations governing harbor siltation. *Regime: two Belgian tidal harbor basins.* [direct_finding, field] (J. Vanlede 2014, [doi:10.1007/s10236-014-0767-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10236-014-0767-9))
- **C474.** In the tidal James River, eroded muddy-bed aggregates were 50-270 times larger than disaggregated sediment and were modelled in incipient suspension or bedload, materially changing predictions of navigation-channel infill. *Regime: tidal James River navigable waterway.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Perkey 2020, [doi:10.1061/(asce)ww.1943-5460.0000578](https://doi.org/10.1061/(asce)ww.1943-5460.0000578))
- **C475.** At Zeebrugge, suspended-mud import occurred from about two hours before high water to high water and was 2-4 times larger at spring than neap tide, while constant dredging permitted spring accumulation and winter deposits were thicker and less dense. *Regime: Port of Zeebrugge harbor basin and docks.* [direct_finding, field] (J. Vanlede 2019, [doi:10.1007/s10236-019-01273-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10236-019-01273-3))
- **C476.** At the Port of Gladstone, combined particle-size, elemental, and isotope tracers differentiated candidate sources, with beryllium-7 indicating recent channel deposition from mudflats or catchments. *Regime: Port of Gladstone navigation channels and catchment, mudflat, and littoral sources.* [direct_finding, field] (Emma L. Jackson 2019, [doi:10.3390/jmse7120434](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse7120434))
- **C477.** For the sediment-choked Dinh An route and Duyen Hai harbor bottlenecks, coupled MIKE 21 simulations compared five structural layouts using water level, velocity, and bed change to identify a preferred channel-stabilization option. *Regime: Dinh An estuary and Duyen Hai harbor basin, Vietnam.* [direct_finding, numerical] (Viet Thanh Nguyen 2024, [doi:10.4274/jems.2023.70446](https://doi.org/10.4274/jems.2023.70446))
- **C1465.** A meta-analysis identifies suspended sediment, contamination, hydraulic entrainment, and underwater noise as distinct dredging stressors affecting fish. *Regime: A critical analysis of the direct effects of dredging on fish.* [literature_review_statement, review] (Amelia Wenger 2017, [doi:10.1111/faf.12218](https://doi.org/10.1111/faf.12218))
- **C1466.** Field monitoring around offshore dredging and beach nourishment documents spatial changes in turbidity, suspended sediment, and water-quality conditions. *Regime: Turbidity and Other Effects Resulting from Trafalgar Sandbank Dredging and Palmar Beach Nourishment.* [direct_finding, field] (Jorge Román‐Sierra 2011, [doi:10.1061/(asce)ww.1943-5460.0000098](https://doi.org/10.1061/(asce)ww.1943-5460.0000098))
- **C1467.** Scale experiments and global examples show that sea-level rise and navigation dredging interact to alter estuary morphology and enhance channel-bend migration. *Regime: Effects of Sea‐Level Rise on Dredging and Dredged Estuary Morphology.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Jana Cox 2022, [doi:10.1029/2022jf006790](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022jf006790))
- **C1468.** Analysis of Mar del Plata Harbor identifies recurrent access-channel and basin sedimentation as a controlling constraint on port development. *Regime: Analysis of Sedimentation Problems at the Entrance to Mar del Plata Harbor.* [direct_finding, mixed] (Raúl A. Cáceres 2016, [doi:10.2112/jcoastres-d-14-00056.1](https://doi.org/10.2112/jcoastres-d-14-00056.1))
- **C1606.** The Port of Newcastle case documents maintenance-dredging and unconfined sea-disposal practice in a high-throughput river port, linking navigability requirements to sediment-management and environmental monitoring. *Regime: PORT OF NEWCASTLE, NSW, AUSTRALIA – MAINTENANCE DREDGING AND UNCONFINED SEA DISPOSAL OF DREDGE MATERIAL.* [direct_finding, field] (Britton 2023, [doi:10.9753/icce.v37.management.68](https://doi.org/10.9753/icce.v37.management.68))
