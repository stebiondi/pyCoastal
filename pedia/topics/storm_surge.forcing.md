## Synthesis

**Well established.** Storm surge is the dynamical coastal response to wind stress and atmospheric pressure acting on a preconditioned ocean, modified by storm size/track/speed, tides, waves, shelf and channel geometry, bathymetry, bottom friction, remote ocean signals, and coastal wetting/drying.

**Governing physics.** Alongshore/onshore wind piles water and drives Ekman/coastal currents; pressure gradients create inverse-barometer and dynamic response; broad storms increase fetch/duration; shelf waves and remote forcing create forerunners; tides change depth/friction; waves add setup and stress feedback; geometry controls convergence and resonance.

**Dimensionless parameters.** Controls include storm radius/shelf width, translation speed/gravity or shelf-wave speed, wind direction/coast orientation, pressure deficit, drag coefficient, bottom-friction parameter, depth/resolution, Rossby and Froude numbers, tidal phase/surge duration, wave setup/surge, channel convergence, and forcing/forecast error.

**Major equations.** Models solve shallow-water or primitive continuity/momentum with Coriolis, pressure-gradient, wind stress, atmospheric pressure, bottom drag and advection; coupled systems add wave radiation stress and sea-state drag; wetting/drying maps inundation; assimilation updates state; sensitivity and surrogate models map forcing to response.

**Typical methods.** Methods include gauge/buoy/wind/pressure validation, historical hindcasts, idealized storm perturbations, global and nested unstructured grids, coupled SWAN–ADCIRC or atmosphere–wave–ocean models, 2-D/3-D comparison, forcing-product and drag/friction sensitivity, data assimilation, ensemble forecasts, process attribution, and AI emulation.

**Numerical models.** Models include ADCIRC v55 and data assimilation, SWAN–ADCIRC, COAWST-style coupling, global storm-tide reanalysis, pan-European forecasting, high-resolution cyclone forecasts, coupled atmosphere–wave–ocean models, 2-D/3-D surge models, subgrid inundation, deep-learning wind/pressure emulators and process indices.

**Experimental datasets.** Evidence includes Gustav, Ike, Rita, Sandy-era systems, Bejisa, Gloria, Haiyan-region islands, Irma, Pearl River typhoons, Bangladesh cyclones, Super Typhoon Fung-Wong, Baltic and Italian storms, Gulf testbeds, Caribbean islands, Wilmington channels and global reanalyses.

**Validated ranges.** The corpus spans local channels and islands to pan-European/global models, extratropical and tropical cyclones, deep ocean to reef/estuary/urban coast, hindcast through operational forecast, local and remote forerunners, multiple mesh/friction/forcing products, and historical to recent events.

**Recent advances.** Recent work uses high-resolution global forecasts, scalable unstructured meshes, component attribution, low-frequency state correction, deep-learning wind/pressure emulation, geometry/bathymetry-aware surge indices, island-specific wind sensitivity, in-situ super-typhoon constraints and shelfbreak-jet attribution.

**Disagreements.** Peak intensity alone poorly predicts surge when storm size and track differ; finer grids improve geometry but may expose friction/bathymetry error; 2-D and 3-D models differ where stratification or vertical stress matters; coupled waves may amplify or redistribute surge; data assimilation can correct state but mask forcing bias.

**Limitations.** Cyclone wind/pressure cores are sparsely observed; reanalysis resolution is limited; drag at extreme winds is uncertain; bathymetry, topography and friction covary; gauges miss spatial maxima; wetting/drying and wave coupling add numerical sensitivity; remote ocean and low-frequency baselines are imperfect.

**Open questions.** Needs include extreme-wind drag saturation, pressure–wind covariance, storm-size/translation priors, remote shelf and ocean-current contributions, wave–tide–surge coupling, morphology and sea-level change, probabilistic forcing ensembles, data assimilation attribution, and physically constrained AI.

**Seminal papers.** The branch progresses from storm-size and event hindcasts to coupled wave–surge testbeds, mesh/friction/forcing sensitivities, global storm-tide systems, remote forerunners, channel-deepening effects, state assimilation, coupled atmosphere feedback, geometry-aware indices and AI forcing emulators.
