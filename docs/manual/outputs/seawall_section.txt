Warnings
  - h* = 0.10 is below 0.23, so the conditions are impulsive. The non-impulsive EurOtop formula used for the crest level understates the discharge, and Goda understates the load; check against the impulsive formulae before fixing the section.
  - The 2.0 m toe berm sits close to the wall, where the standing wave moves the bed least, which is why Tanimoto allows 0.08 t stone. The scour hole forms about a quarter wavelength out, 11 m from the face; widen the berm to reach it if the toe is also the scour protection, and the stone will grow.
  - The wall retains 8.4 m. A cantilever L-wall is ordinary practice up to about 8 m; beyond that a counterforted wall, a caisson or an anchored sheet-pile wall is the usual form, and the stem here (1.00 m) shows why.
  - Pore water is 64% of the pressure on the back of the wall. A drain holding the water table at still water level would cut the total from 548 to about 403 kN/m.
  - The drawdown case governs: the wall is sized by the saturated backfill pushing it seaward at the trough, so the backfill grading and the drainage detail set the base width more than the design wave does.
